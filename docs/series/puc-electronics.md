## Breadboard canon (keep identical across episodes)
- Schematic: ink #16213E strokes 4 px, standard symbols (IEC-style resistor zigzag as in the style frame, battery long plate = +). Wires draw on stroke by stroke.
- Dots on wires = ELECTRONS, amber #E8A317 with an ink outline when on white, speed proportional to current, moving from - to + outside the source. Conventional current, when shown, is a violet #7A4FE0 arrow labelled I. The exam trap of Ep 1 sets this rule; never mix them up later.
- Symbol colours (diagram, KaTeX, readout use the same): electrons / count n amber #E8A317 · current I violet #7A4FE0 · voltage V teal #0E8F8F · charge e and Q orange #D9731A · time t and resistance R ink #16213E. Scope CH1 (input) light blue-grey #8FA3C8, CH2 (output) amber.
- Oscilloscope panel: dark navy screen #16213E with a 6 x 4 division grid #2A3758, channel labels in Plex Mono, traces 4 px, drawn live (sweep) then scrolling at constant speed.
- Constants (NCERT): e = 1.6 × 10^-19 C (NCERT gives 1.602 × 10^-19 C). Electronics definition used: the branch of science and engineering that deals with the controlled flow of electrons through a vacuum, a gas or a semiconductor.
- Verified history (Ep 1): Fleming valve 1904 (US patent filed 16 Nov 1904) · de Forest Audion (triode) 1906 · ENIAC completed 1946, 17,468 vacuum tubes, about 27 tonnes · point-contact transistor 23 Dec 1947, Bardeen, Brattain and Shockley · Kilby's integrated circuit 12 Sep 1958 · first commercial microprocessor 1971 (4-bit). Sources: Wikipedia (Fleming valve), historyofinformation.com, Guinness World Records, PBS A Science Odyssey, National MagLab.
