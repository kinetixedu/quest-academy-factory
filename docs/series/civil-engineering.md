## Who it is for
Any B.E./B.Tech Civil student in India, whatever the university or state. The list merges the AICTE model curriculum, GATE CE, JNTUH R22, VTU 2022, Anna University, SPPU 2019 and Mumbai University. Never mention exam weightage or a university's marks. Common first-year subjects (Maths, Physics, Chemistry, Programming, English, Basic Electrical) are not in this series.

## The running story (canon - keep it consistent)
**The New School at Hosahalli** - Hosahalli (made-up town) in Karnataka. The old government school leaks every monsoon and a wall has cracked. A young engineer builds a new two-storey school for 600 children, from the empty plot to handover day. Every civil engineering idea solves the next real problem on that project.
- **Ananya** - 24, site engineer, first big project, leads the build. Careful, asks "show me the numbers". Yellow hi-vis vest over a blue shirt, white helmet.
- **Raghu** - 52, head mason and site supervisor, 30 years on sites. Trusts his hands, slowly learns to trust drawings; always right about workmanship. Khaki shirt, towel on the shoulder, measuring tape.
- **Farhan** - 22, surveyor and draftsman. Loves instruments, the total station and the drone; draws every plan. Navy jacket, laptop bag.
- **Mrs. Lakshmi** - 50, headmistress, the client. Thinks only of the children; always asks "will it be safe?". Green saree, reading glasses on a chain.
- **Prof. Deshpande** - 62, retired structures professor, Ananya's mentor and design checker. Answers with one sharp question. Grey hair, round glasses, white kurta.
- Children appear as unnamed pupils. Workers are unnamed.
- Growth arc, in order:
  1. Year 1 (Eps 1-19): the brief and the plan. The cracked old school; what civil engineers do; parts of a building; plot rules and bye-laws; the loads the new school must carry; forces, supports, trusses, friction and centroids worked out on the assembly-hall roof, the gate, the ramp and the water tank; Farhan's drawings (plan, elevation, section); choosing bricks, stone, timber, steel and green materials.
  2. Year 2 (Eps 20-86): breaking ground. Testing steel and checking beams (strength of materials); water to the site and the drains (fluid mechanics); surveying the plot; the rock and soil under it; making good concrete; walls, stairs, roof and waterproofing going up; the first structural checks.
  3. Year 3 (Eps 87-170): the full design. Frames and continuous beams; RCC slabs, beams, columns and footings; the steel roof over the assembly hall; soil tests and foundations; the road, bus bay and junction outside the school; rainfall and runoff on the site; drinking water, toilets, sewage, air and waste for 600 children.
  4. Year 4 (Eps 171-215): finishing and beyond. The school garden canal and the town's check dam; the children's rail and airport field trips; estimates, tenders and the schedule; safety and quality on site; prestress and earthquake checks; repair of the old block; green building, BIM and handover day (Ep 215 ends the series).
- Never introduce real brands into the story (no real cement, steel, paint, software or machine brands). Real engineers and scientists (Hooke, Euler, Bernoulli, Mohr, Terzaghi, Darcy, Castigliano, Visvesvaraya...) appear only as text or quote cards with verified facts and dates.
- Indian Standard and IRC codes are named only where verified (number and year).
- Workplace examples ("At work today") are generic, with no brand names: a metro line, a hospital, a highway project, a housing society, a dam, a bridge, an airport, a water board.

## Year 1 beats (the story problem each episode solves; numbers are EXAMPLE FIGURES unless verified)
2 Building Components: Mrs. Lakshmi asks why the old wall cracked; Ananya walks her from footing, plinth and plinth beam up through columns, beams, slab, walls, lintels, sunshades, parapet and stairs of the new two-storey school.
3 Building Planning and Bye-laws: Farhan fits 12 classrooms on the plot; setbacks, ground coverage and FAR/FSI decide how big the school may be; orientation for light and breeze; rules differ by town, so they check the local bye-laws and the National Building Code.
4 Types of Loads: what will push on the school - its own weight (dead), children, desks and crowds (imposed), wind, earthquake, rain water on the roof; IS 875 parts and IS 1893 named only once verified.
5 Forces, Resultants and Moments: two ropes lift a precast lintel; forces add tip to tail; the heavy new gate - why the handle sits far from the hinge.
6 Free Body Diagrams and Equilibrium: the lamp over the gate hangs from two wires; Lami's theorem; Raghu's ladder.
7 Supports, Beams and Reactions: the corridor beam on two columns; pinned, roller and fixed supports; reactions under a point load and a spread load.
8 Trusses: the assembly hall roof truss; method of joints and method of sections; which members pull and which push.
9 Friction: the wheelchair ramp and its slope; the ladder against the wall; why a dusty ramp is dangerous.
10 Centroid and Centre of Gravity: the L-shaped name wall and the T-shaped beam; where the balance point sits.
11 Moment of Inertia: Raghu's plank sags flat but stays stiff on edge; why beams are deep, not wide; parallel axis theorem.
12 Work, Energy and Momentum: the brick hoist lifting a load to the roof; the pile hammer; power of the hoist motor.
13 Drawing Basics: Farhan's sheet - scales (1:100, 1:50), line types, lettering, dimensioning.
14 Orthographic Projections: the staircase block in front view, top view and side view; first angle method.
15 Isometric Projections: Farhan draws the water tank stand in 3D so Mrs. Lakshmi can picture it.
16 Building Drawing: one classroom as plan, elevation and section - the drawings Raghu builds from.
17 Stones and Bricks: choosing bricks for the walls; field tests and lab tests (strength, water absorption, efflorescence); stone for the plinth.
18 Lime, Timber, Glass and Plastics: doors and window frames (seasoned timber), glass for light, PVC pipes, lime plaster in the old block.
19 Steel and Green Materials: TMT bars for the frame; fly ash bricks and AAC blocks; why a school should be built green. Next is Year 2 (Ep 20 Stress and Strain).

## Canon (update after every batch)
- Ep 01: the old Hosahalli school was built decades ago; roof leaks every monsoon, a diagonal crack runs over a classroom door. The new school: two storeys, 600 children, on an empty plot next to the old one. Ananya is the site engineer, Raghu the head mason, Farhan the surveyor-draftsman, Mrs. Lakshmi the headmistress (client), Prof. Deshpande the checker.
- Eps 12-15 (EXAMPLE FIGURES): classroom 7.5 m x 6 m. Brick hoist rated 5 kN, roof at 7 m, 20 s a trip, 2.5 kW motor input at 70% efficiency; Ananya's painted limit "5 kN, no one stands below". A river bridge outside Hosahalli uses a 1 t pile hammer. Farhan's scales: plot 1:500, plans 1:100, door details 1:50; every sheet signed by Ananya, says "do not scale", names its views and carries the first angle symbol. Staircase: 150 mm rise, 300 mm tread, 1200 mm wide. Water tank stand: 3 m tall, 2 m x 2 m base, four legs braced at half height, ladder and railing; tank on top 2 x 2 x 1.5 m (6,000 litres). Isometric views go to the client, orthographic views go to site.
- Eps 7-11 (EXAMPLE FIGURES): corridor beam 6 m span on columns A (pinned) and B (roller); 30 kN water tank 2 m from A (RA 20, RB 10 kN); slab adds 10 kN/m. Every classroom window has a cantilever sunshade. Assembly hall: 12 m clear span, no internal columns, steel king post trusses with 3 m rise and 20 kN at the peak (rafter 22.36 kN compression, tie 20 kN tension, king post zero but kept). Gate ramp 1:12 with a resting landing, rough finish swept daily. Raghu's ladder rule: 1 m out for every 4 m up. Name wall at the gate: L-shaped (3 x 1 m base plus 1 x 2 m pillar), centroid (1.1, 1.1) m, footing centred under the whole wall. T beam: 400 x 100 mm flange, 200 x 300 mm web, centroid 230 mm from the bottom. Classroom beams 230 x 450 mm. Raghu's arc: after the plank demo (Ep 11) he trusts the calculations.
