## The running story (canon - keep it consistent)
**Laddoo Lab** - Zoya turns her Nani's millet laddoo recipe into a snack brand in Bengaluru. Viewers watch a real brand grow from a home kitchen to shops and online, one marketing idea at a time.
- **Zoya** - founder, 22, final-year college student. Curious, makes the decisions, learns marketing as she goes.
- **Nani** - Zoya's grandmother. Owns the recipe, guards taste and quality ("no shortcuts").
- **Arjun** - Zoya's older brother. Sales: talks to shopkeepers, handles deliveries and money.
- **Dev** - Zoya's college friend. Design and social media: logo, packs, posts, ads.
- **Mrs. Rao** - a busy working mother and Laddoo Lab's first regular customer; the face of the target customer (school tiffin buyer).
- Product: healthy millet laddoos (ragi and jaggery, no refined sugar). Later flavours: cocoa-ragi, coconut-jowar. Later formats: a 6-pack for tiffin boxes, a festive gift box.
- Growth arc, in order:
  1. Eps 1-6: home kitchen, selling to neighbours and Zoya's college; learning what marketing, needs, value and the environment mean.
  2. Eps 7-12: research and STP - surveys at school gates, gyms and offices; segments (tiffin parents, gym-goers, office snackers); target = busy parents; position = "the healthy sweet for tiffin boxes".
  3. Eps 13-19: the mix and the product - the 6-pack, product levels, life cycle, the cocoa-ragi launch (Ep 18), the product range (Ep 19).
  4. Eps 20-22: branding - name, logo, colours, brand equity.
  5. Eps 23-25: pricing.
  6. Eps 26-27: distribution - 10 local stores, then a supermarket chain and an online shop.
  7. Eps 28-31: communication - campaign, a festive offer, Arjun's shop pitch, a newspaper story about Nani.
  8. Eps 32-35: planning and control, customer relationships, customer lifetime value (Mrs. Rao), the yearly scorecard.
- Never introduce real brands into the story. Real thinkers are fine with correct facts and dates, for example Philip Kotler, Neil Borden ("marketing mix", 1964), E. Jerome McCarthy (4Ps, 1960), Booms and Bitner (7Ps, 1981), Theodore Levitt ("Marketing Myopia", 1960), Peter Drucker, Abraham Maslow. Verify each before use.
- Workplace examples ("At work today") are generic, with no brand names, for example a phone maker, a bank, a food delivery app, a bakery, a hospital, a school, an airline.
- Illustrative numbers are labelled with the ghost chip "EXAMPLE FIGURES".
