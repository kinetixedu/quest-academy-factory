## The running story (canon - keep it consistent)
**Laddoo Lab** - Zoya turns her Nani's millet laddoo recipe into a snack brand in Bengaluru. Viewers watch a real brand grow from a home kitchen to shops and online, one marketing idea at a time.
- **Zoya** - founder, 22, final-year college student. Curious, makes the decisions, learns marketing as she goes.
- **Nani** - Zoya's grandmother. Owns the recipe, guards taste and quality ("no shortcuts").
- **Arjun** - Zoya's older brother. Sales: talks to shopkeepers, handles deliveries and money.
- **Dev** - Zoya's college friend. Design and social media: logo, packs, posts, ads.
- **Mrs. Rao** - a busy working mother and Laddoo Lab's first regular customer; the face of the target customer (school tiffin buyer).
- Product: healthy millet laddoos (ragi and jaggery, no refined sugar). Later flavours: cocoa-ragi, coconut-jowar. Later formats: a 6-pack for tiffin boxes, a festive gift box.
- Growth arc, in order:
  1. Eps 1-6: home kitchen, selling to neighbours and Zoya's college; learning what marketing, needs, value and the environment mean.
  2. Eps 7-12: research and STP - surveys at school gates, gyms and offices; segments (tiffin parents, gym-goers, office snackers); target = busy parents; position = "the healthy sweet for tiffin boxes".
  3. Eps 13-19: the mix and the product - the 6-pack, product levels, life cycle, the cocoa-ragi launch (Ep 18), the product range (Ep 19).
  4. Eps 20-22: branding - name, logo, colours, brand equity.
  5. Eps 23-25: pricing.
  6. Eps 26-27: distribution - 10 local stores, then a supermarket chain and an online shop.
  7. Eps 28-31: communication - campaign, a festive offer, Arjun's shop pitch, a newspaper story about Nani.
  8. Eps 32-35: planning and control, customer relationships, customer lifetime value (Mrs. Rao), the yearly scorecard.
- Never introduce real brands into the story. Real thinkers are fine with correct facts and dates, for example Philip Kotler, Neil Borden ("marketing mix", 1964), E. Jerome McCarthy (4Ps, 1960), Booms and Bitner (7Ps, 1981), Theodore Levitt ("Marketing Myopia", 1960), Peter Drucker, Abraham Maslow. Verify each before use.
- Workplace examples ("At work today") are generic, with no brand names, for example a phone maker, a bank, a food delivery app, a bakery, a hospital, a school, an airline.
- Illustrative numbers are labelled with the ghost chip "EXAMPLE FIGURES".

## Canon added by Eps 22-26
- Pack is navy and gold. Ghee is in the recipe.
- Cost per 6-pack ₹80 (ingredients 45, box and label 15, gas and electricity 8, delivery 12); 50% markup -> ₹120. Parents value it at about ₹140.
- Student price ₹100 with a college ID (1 pack per ID per day). Monthly 4-pack ₹440 for everyone. Regular 6-pack stays ₹120.
- Stores keep 20% (₹24), Laddoo Lab gets ₹96 per pack. 10 local stores; a nearby office orders 4 packs a month; supermarket chain + Dev's online shop start in Ep 26.

## Canon added by Eps 32-35
- About 3,000-3,450 packs a month; the year closes at 39,000 packs (30,000 the year before). Plans checked monthly; Arjun refills the supermarket shelf weekly.
- Dev keeps a customer book; one Saturday reminder a week, only with consent. Regulars get every tenth pack free and a Sunday delivery.
- Mrs. Rao orders on Sundays, buys 40 school weeks a year; lapsed two Sundays in July, won back with a coconut-jowar trial pack, referred two friends.
- Pack profit ₹40 (₹120 - ₹80). Cost to win a new parent ₹500. 200 regular families, 170 kept. Festive campaign cost ₹40,000, brought ₹1 lakh extra profit. NPS +50.

## Canon added by Eps 7-11
- Before the information system: 24 packs a day; demand about 40 on Fridays, 15 on Mondays. After: unsold packs drop from 20 to 4 a week.
- Survey: 150 people, 50 each at school gates, gyms, offices. Segments: tiffin parents 60, gym-goers 40, office snackers 50. Tiffins packed by mothers ~50%, fathers ~30%, grandparents ~20% (so "tiffin parents", never "mothers").
- Target: busy parents (concentrated); office snackers kept as a later second group.
- Positioning statement: "To busy parents who want a healthy tiffin snack, Laddoo Lab is the healthy sweet for tiffin boxes, that is made with ragi and jaggery and has no refined sugar."

## Canon added by Eps 12-16
- The 6-pack = six small, individually wrapped laddoos. The first try was an 18-laddoo jar at ₹360 sold from Nani's kitchen - a flop.
- Monday deliveries: Sunday 8 PM reminder; Nani checks each pack at 6:00; Arjun leaves 6:40 with a route card and a Laddoo Lab cap; sealed pack + photo message by 7:20 (school bus 7:30). Every pack carries a card from Nani.
- Dev's posters and free tastes at school gates.
- A rival home kitchen sells millet laddoos at ₹120 for six (Ep 16). Future idea: a tiffin plan with a new flavour every month.
- Laddoo Lab buys ragi and jaggery (materials), a grinder (capital item), tape and gas (supplies).

## Canon added by Eps 17-21
- Ep 17: a neighbourhood bakery starts selling millet laddoos (rival, alongside the Ep 16 home kitchen). The 6-pack is at maturity.
- Ep 18: ideas dropped - mango, "sugar free", spicy masala; protein bar lost the concept test. Concept test with 20 parents: cocoa-ragi 12, coconut-jowar 6 (saved), protein bar 2. Cocoa-ragi ₹120 for six; 40 families tested it two weeks, 32 reordered; Nani cut the cocoa before launch.
- Ep 19 range (5 products): original ragi 6-pack (cash cow), cocoa-ragi 6-pack (star), coconut-jowar 6-pack for gym-goers (question mark, waits), festive gift box for Diwali (question mark, funded), loose big laddoos at the college stall (dog, kept as a first taste).
- Ep 20 brand: logo a gold laddoo with a small navy leaf; navy (trust), jaggery gold (warmth), cream (home-made); tagline "The healthy sweet for tiffin boxes"; navy packs, cream LADDOO LAB wordmark, flavour band gold/cocoa brown/cream. Nani's rule: say only what is true.
- Ep 21: identity healthy, honest, home-made. School-gate survey of 30 parents; gaps "expensive", "for grandparents". Pack now shows "₹20 a laddoo"; Dev posts kids at lunch with ingredients; Nani stays in the posts.

## Canon added by Eps 2-6
- Paper packs instead of plastic (₹4 vs ₹2 a pack, illustrative); price stays ₹120.
- Early on Arjun delivers to Mrs. Rao's door by 7:00 AM; Nani adds handwritten notes; Mrs. Rao recommends Laddoo Lab to two other mothers.
- Nani buys jaggery in bulk straight from a farmer (jaggery went ₹60 to ₹80 a kilo). The pack says "made with ragi millet". A new bakery near the college is a rival. Parents pay by UPI.
