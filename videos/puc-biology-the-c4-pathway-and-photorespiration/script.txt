L1|0.35|Now follow carbon dioxide through the C4 pathway, step by step.
L2|0.75|Step one. In a mesophyll cell, carbon dioxide joins PEP, a three carbon molecule. The enzyme is PEP carboxylase, or PEPcase.
L3|0.75|Step two. This makes OAA, the four carbon acid. It changes into other four carbon acids, like malic acid or aspartic acid.
L4|0.75|Step three. These acids travel into the bundle sheath cells.
L5|0.75|Step four. There they are broken down. They release carbon dioxide and a three carbon molecule.
L6|0.75|Step five. The carbon dioxide enters the Calvin cycle, run by RuBisCO. The three carbon molecule goes back to the mesophyll and becomes PEP again.
T1|0.6|Remember this. C4 plants pump carbon dioxide into the bundle sheath.
C1|0.35|Why pump it? Because RuBisCO has a weakness.
C2|0.45|The active site of RuBisCO can bind carbon dioxide or oxygen. Which one it grabs depends on how much of each is around.
C3|0.65|When oxygen binds instead, RuBP splits into one PGA and one phosphoglycolate, which has two carbons. This is photorespiration.
D1|0.35|Photorespiration is a waste. Here is why.
D2|0.45|It makes no sugar and no ATP. Instead, it uses up ATP and lets carbon dioxide escape.
D3|0.65|In C4 plants, the bundle sheath is packed with carbon dioxide. So RuBisCO grabs carbon dioxide, and photorespiration does not happen. That is why C4 plants give better yields.
U1|0.35|Let's put C3 and C4 plants side by side.
U2|0.45|C3 plants, like wheat, fix carbon dioxide once, with RuBisCO in mesophyll cells. Their first product is PGA. Their leaves have no Kranz anatomy.
U3|0.6|C4 plants, like maize and sorghum, fix it twice. First PEPcase in the mesophyll, then RuBisCO in the bundle sheath. Their first product is OAA.

U5|0.65|No, it does not! So much carbon dioxide is packed there that RuBisCO grabs carbon dioxide, not oxygen.
