L1|0.35|This score is the oxidation number. It is the charge an atom would have, if every shared pair went to the stronger puller.
L2|0.4|It is a pretend charge, a bookkeeping score. But it lets us track electrons anywhere.
L3|0.65|Now redox becomes easy. Oxidation means the oxidation number goes up. Reduction means it goes down.
D1|0.35|Now the main rules. Step by step.
D2|0.75|Rule one. An element on its own scores zero. Like hydrogen gas, oxygen gas, or sodium metal.
D3|0.75|Rule two. A simple ion scores its charge. Sodium plus is plus one. Chloride is minus one.
D4|0.75|Rule three. All the scores in a molecule add to zero. In an ion, they add to the ion's charge.
R1|0.35|Then a few fixed scores to remember.
R2|0.4|Fluorine is always minus one. Group one metals are plus one. Group two metals are plus two.
R3|0.4|Hydrogen is plus one, but minus one when joined to metals, like in sodium hydride.
R4|0.6|Oxygen is minus two. But it is minus one in peroxides, minus half in superoxides, and positive when joined to fluorine.
