G1|0.35|Feature one: growth.
G2|0.45|Growth means two things together: more body mass, and more cells or more individuals.
G3|0.45|A many celled body grows by cell division. One cell becomes two, two become four.
G4|0.45|Plants keep growing this way all their life. Animals grow only up to a certain age.
G5|0.45|After that, cell division still replaces worn out cells, like the skin you shed.
G6|0.45|But mountains, boulders and sand mounds grow too, by piling material on their surface.

R1|0.35|Feature two: reproduction, making young ones like the parents.
R2|0.45|Many living things do it without a partner. Yeast and hydra grow a bud that breaks away.
R3|0.45|Cut a flatworm called Planaria into pieces, and each piece grows into a whole new worm. That is regeneration.
R4|0.45|Fungi, threadlike algae and the protonema, the young thread stage of a moss, break into pieces, and each piece lives on. That is fragmentation.
R5|0.45|In one celled living things, like bacteria, growing and reproducing are the same event: one cell divides into two.
R6|0.65|But mules, worker bees and couples who cannot have children never reproduce, and they are alive. So reproduction fails.
M1|0.35|Feature three: metabolism.
M2|0.45|Metabolism is the sum of all the chemical reactions in a body. Food is broken down, new parts are built.
M3|0.45|Every living thing runs metabolism, and no non living object does.
M4|0.65|But scientists can run one of these reactions in a test tube, with no cell at all. That tube is neither living nor non living. It is a living reaction.
C1|0.45|So the real line is drawn by feature four: cellular organisation. Life happens inside organised cells.
C2|0.45|And one more feature: response. Every living thing senses its surroundings and reacts.
C3|0.45|A shoot bends toward light. Day length tells many plants and animals when to breed.
C4|0.65|Humans go further. We are aware of ourselves. That is self consciousness.
