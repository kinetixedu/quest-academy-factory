H5|0.6|To answer that, we need the rules of equilibrium.
P1|0.35|A rigid body is in equilibrium when nothing about its motion changes.
P2|0.35|First, its centre of mass does not speed up. That needs the total force to be zero.
P3|0.4|Second, it does not start to turn faster. That needs the total torque to be zero.
P4|0.6|Both rules at once. Zero force, and zero torque.

S5|0.4|Each weight times its lever arm gives a torque. Sixty g times two is one hundred and twenty g. Thirty g times two is only sixty g.
S6|0.4|Now the friend moves in. At one point five metres, it still tips left.

D1|0.35|Let's write the rules, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. Translational equilibrium: the vector sum of all forces is zero.
D3|0.75|Step two. Rotational equilibrium: the vector sum of all torques is zero, about any point.
D4|0.75|Step three. For the see-saw, the pivot pushes up with N. So N equals the two weights added: ninety g.
D5|0.75|Step four. Torques about the pivot: W one d one equals W two d two. Anticlockwise equals clockwise.
D6|0.75|Step five. This is the principle of moments. For a lever, load times load arm equals effort times effort arm.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Equilibrium needs zero total force, and zero total torque.
R1|0.35|So where does the heavy friend sit? Link by link.
R2|0.4|Diya's torque is thirty g times two. Sixty g metres.
R3|0.4|The friend weighs twice as much, so needs only half the lever arm.
R4|0.6|Half of two metres is one metre. Twice the weight sits at half the distance.

K1|0.4|Side by side. Two equal, opposite pushes along the same line. The forces cancel, and nothing turns.
