C1|0.35|To answer that, we start with electric charge.
C2|0.35|Charge is a property of tiny particles. Protons carry positive charge. Electrons carry negative charge.
C3|0.65|Like charges push each other away. Unlike charges pull together.
C4|0.35|Charge is measured in coulombs, written with a capital C.
C5|0.35|And charge comes in fixed packets. The smallest packet is the charge of one electron, e. That is one point six times ten to the minus nineteen coulombs.
C6|0.65|So any charge Q is a whole number of packets. Q equals n times e. One coulomb is about six point two five times ten to the eighteen electrons.
I1|0.35|Now, current.
I2|0.35|When charge moves along a wire, we get an electric current.
I3|0.35|Stand at one point and count the charge that passes every second. That rate is the current.
I4|0.7|So I equals Q divided by t.
I5|0.65|Current is measured in amperes. One ampere means one coulomb passing every second.
I6|0.65|Remember from episode one. Electrons drift from minus to plus. Conventional current I points the other way.
P1|0.35|But why do the charges move at all? They need a push.
P2|0.35|Think of two water tanks joined by a pipe. Water flows only when one tank is higher than the other.
P3|0.35|In a circuit, the height is electric potential. Charges flow when there is a potential difference between two points.
P4|0.35|Potential difference is the work done to move one coulomb of charge from one point to the other.
P5|0.7|So V equals W divided by Q.
P6|0.65|It is measured in volts. One volt means one joule of work for every coulomb.
P7|0.65|And voltage is simply the everyday name for potential difference.
B1|0.35|Let's watch both on the breadboard.
B2|0.35|One cell, one switch, and a resistor. A voltmeter reads the voltage across the cell.
B3|0.35|With the switch open, the cell still has one point five volts across it. But no current flows.
B4|0.65|Close the switch. Now the charges move. The meter reads zero point one five amperes.
B5|0.35|Add a second cell. The push doubles to three volts.
B6|0.35|Watch the scope. Both traces step up together. The electrons speed up, and the current rises to zero point three amperes.
B7|0.65|More push, more flow. And with no push, there is no flow at all.
T1|0.8|Voltage is the push. Current is the flow.
D1|0.35|Now, how much energy does the source give?
D2|0.7|From the volt, the work W equals V times Q.
D3|0.7|And the charge Q equals I times t.
D4|0.7|Put them together. W equals V times I times t.
D5|0.65|Volts times amperes times seconds gives joules, the unit of energy.
