D1|0.35|Now the rule, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. To fill the store slowly, your push balances the force. Your force is minus F.
D3|0.75|Step two. Your work is stored. So delta U equals minus the integral of F d x.
D4|0.75|Step three. For one tiny step, d U equals minus F d x.
D5|0.75|Step four. Divide by d x. F equals minus d U by d x.
D6|0.75|Check with gravity, up positive. U equals m g h, so d U by d h is m g, and F is minus m g. Downwards. It works.
T1|0.7|Remember this. The force is minus the slope of U. It always pushes toward lower U.
R1|0.35|Why the minus sign? Follow the links.
R2|0.4|One. Where U rises as x increases, the slope is positive.
R3|0.4|Two. The minus sign makes F negative there. The force points back, toward lower U.
R4|0.4|Three. Where U falls as x increases, F is positive. Again toward lower U.
R5|0.6|Four. Where the slope is flat, the force is zero. Like a ball resting at the bottom of a bowl.
