I1|0.4|An interval is a set of real numbers on the number line, between two ends, a and b, where a is less than b.
I2|0.4|It holds every real number in between. Not just whole numbers. Twenty point five, thirty one point two, all of them.
I3|0.65|So an interval is an infinite set, and it is a subset of R, the real numbers.
O1|0.4|The open interval, a to b, uses round brackets. It is every x with a less than x, and x less than b. Both ends are left out.
O2|0.6|On the number line, we draw its ends as hollow circles.
O3|0.4|The closed interval, a to b, uses square brackets. Now a and b are both included. a is less than or equal to x, and x is less than or equal to b.
O4|0.6|Its ends are drawn as filled dots.
