S1|0.4|But a picture is not a proof. NCERT uses the sandwich theorem.
S2|0.4|If g is always between f and h, and f and h close in on the same number, then g is trapped. It must close in on that number too.
S3|0.65|Think of cheese between two slices of bread. Squeeze the bread together, and the cheese goes with it.
D1|0.35|Here is the proof in small steps, for x between zero and a right angle.
D2|0.75|Step one. Compare three shapes: a small triangle, the sector, and a big triangle. Each one fits inside the next.
D3|0.75|Step two. With radius one, their areas are half sine x, half x, and half tan x. So sine x is less than x, which is less than tan x.
D4|0.75|Step three. Divide by sine x, then flip each part upside down. The signs flip too. We get cos x, less than sine x over x, less than one.
D5|0.65|Step four. As x tends to zero, cos x tends to one. So sine x over x is squeezed between two things that tend to one. Its limit is one.
R1|0.4|Side by side. With x in radians, sine x over x tends to one.
R2|0.6|With x in degrees, it does not. Sine of one degree is about zero point zero one seven five, so the ratio tends to pi over one eighty instead.
