P1|0.35|Thermodynamics is the study of energy, as heat and work, moving from place to place.
P2|0.4|First, pick the part you want to study. Like drawing a chalk circle around your toys on the floor.
P3|0.4|The part inside the circle is the system. Here, the food in the tiffin.
P4|0.4|Everything outside it is the surroundings. The air, the corridor, Aarav's hands.
P5|0.4|The line between them is the boundary. It can be a real wall, like the steel, or an imaginary line.
P6|0.65|System plus surroundings together make the universe.
S1|0.35|Now put a hot gas in a box, on our molecule bench.
S2|0.4|Two things can cross the boundary. Matter, which means the stuff itself. And energy, like heat.
S3|0.4|An open system lets both cross. Steam leaves the open cup, and heat leaves too.
S4|0.4|A closed system stops matter, but lets energy through. That is Aarav's tiffin.
S5|0.4|The steel keeps the food in, but heat flows out into the air. So the food cools.
S6|0.4|An isolated system stops both. A good flask comes close. No steam out, almost no heat out.
S7|0.65|A wall that stops heat is called adiabatic. The flask has nearly adiabatic walls.
V1|0.35|Next, how do we describe a system?
V2|0.4|We use a few things we can measure. Pressure, volume, temperature, and the amount in moles.
V3|0.4|Together they fix the state of the system. Like a photo of it at one moment.
V4|0.4|A state function depends only on the state right now, not on how the system got there.
V5|0.4|Think of climbing a hill. Your height at the top is the same, by the steep path or the long winding path.
V6|0.4|Height is like a state function. But the distance you walked depends on the path.
V7|0.65|Pressure, volume and temperature are state functions. Heat and work depend on the path. They are path functions.
B1|0.35|One more pair. Pour a cup of hot tea into two half cups.
B2|0.4|The volume halves. The mass halves. They depend on how much stuff there is. They are extensive.
B3|0.4|But each half is still just as hot. Temperature does not depend on amount. It is intensive.
B4|3.2|Pause and think. Density is mass divided by volume. Is density extensive or intensive?
B5|0.65|Intensive! Mass halves and volume halves, so their ratio stays the same.
T1|0.7|Remember this. A state function cares only about the start and the end, never the road between.
W1|0.3|Now, one worked example.
W2|0.4|A gas starts at three hundred kelvin and two litres. It ends at three fifty kelvin and five litres.
W3|0.75|Path one. Warm it straight to three fifty kelvin, and let it grow to five litres.
W4|0.75|Path two. Cool it to two fifty kelvin, let it swell to eight litres, then bring it to the same end.
W5|0.75|Change in temperature, delta T, is final minus initial. Three fifty minus three hundred. Plus fifty kelvin, on both paths.
W6|0.75|Change in volume. Five minus two. Plus three litres, on both paths.
W7|0.65|Same start, same end, same change. But the heat used on the two paths is different.
C1|0.35|The ways a system changes have names too.
C2|0.4|Isothermal means constant temperature. Iso means same, and thermal means heat.
C3|0.4|Isobaric means constant pressure. Isochoric means constant volume.
C4|0.4|Adiabatic means no heat goes in or out.
C5|0.65|And cyclic means the system comes back to where it started. So every state function changes by zero.

X3|0.65|No. Closed stops only matter. Energy still crosses. Only an isolated system stops both.
