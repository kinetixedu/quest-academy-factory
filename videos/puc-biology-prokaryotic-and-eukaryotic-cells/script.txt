Z1|0.35|Let's zoom into the prokaryotes.

L1|0.35|Now the eukaryotic cell, step by step.
L2|0.75|Step one. Eukaryotes are the protists, plants, animals and fungi.
L3|0.75|Step two. Their cytoplasm is divided into many rooms by membrane bound organelles. This is called compartmentalisation.
L4|0.75|Step three. They have a well organised nucleus, with a nuclear envelope around it.
L5|0.75|Step four. Their genetic material is organised into chromosomes.
L6|0.75|Step five. They have structures for moving and a cytoskeleton, a frame of fibres that gives the cell its shape.
T1|0.6|Remember this. No nuclear membrane, prokaryotic. A true nucleus, eukaryotic.
C1|0.35|Now compare them side by side, with one example each.
C2|0.45|A bacterium is prokaryotic. Its DNA is naked, it has no membrane bound organelles, and it has only ribosomes.
C3|0.45|Your cheek cell is eukaryotic. Its DNA sits inside a nucleus, and it has many organelles, like mitochondria, the endoplasmic reticulum and the Golgi complex.
C4|0.65|In eukaryotic cells, ribosomes are found in the cytoplasm, on the rough endoplasmic reticulum, and inside chloroplasts and mitochondria.
D1|0.35|Eukaryotic cells come in two big kinds, plant and animal.

D3|0.45|Animal cells have centrioles, which are absent in almost all plant cells.
