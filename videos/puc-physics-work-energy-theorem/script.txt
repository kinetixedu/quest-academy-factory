P3|0.4|A force that pushes against the motion, like brakes, takes energy out. The body slows down.
P4|0.4|That energy, put in or taken out by a force, is its work.
P5|0.65|So the work done on a body changes its kinetic energy. That is the work-energy theorem.

D1|0.35|Now the proof, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. For a steady force, v squared minus u squared equals two a d.
D3|0.75|Step two. Multiply both sides by half m. Half m v squared, minus half m u squared, equals m a d.
D4|0.75|Step three. By Newton's second law, m a is the net force F. So the right side is F d, the work done.
D5|0.75|Step four. The left side is K final minus K initial.
D6|0.75|So the change in kinetic energy equals the net work done.
T1|0.7|Remember this. The net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy.
