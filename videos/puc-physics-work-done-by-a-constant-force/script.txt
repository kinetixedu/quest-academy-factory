P1|0.35|So what is work in physics?
P2|0.4|Work is done when a force moves something. A force must act, and the thing must move.
P3|0.4|Push a wall all day. It does not move. So you do no work on it, however tired you feel.
P4|0.4|And only the part of the force along the motion counts. That is the shadow from the last episode.
P5|0.65|So work is force, times displacement, times cos theta.
S1|0.35|Let's measure work live. Diya drags a box of books with a rope. She pulls with fifty newtons.
S2|0.4|First, the rope is flat, so theta is zero. Watch the work meter.
S3|0.4|One metre, fifty joules. Two metres, one hundred. Four metres, two hundred joules.
S4|0.4|Now she pulls at sixty degrees. The same fifty newtons, the same four metres.
S5|0.5|This time the meter stops at one hundred joules. Only half her pull is along the floor.
S6|0.35|Slow-mo replay.
S7|0.5|F is along the rope. Its forward part is F cos theta, twenty five newtons. The box moves along d.
S8|0.65|On the graph, work grows in a straight line. The flat rope gives the steeper line.
D1|0.35|Now the formula, one small step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. Work is the forward part of the force, times the displacement. W equals F cos theta, times d.
D3|0.75|Step two. That is exactly a dot product. W equals F dot d.
D4|0.75|Step three. Force and displacement are vectors, but their dot product is a number. So work is a scalar. A size, no direction.
D5|0.75|Step four. One newton, moving something one metre along it, does one joule of work. Its dimensions are M L squared T to the minus two.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Work needs a force and a displacement. Only the part of the force along the motion counts.
C1|0.35|Now, back to Diya's bag. Side by side.
C2|0.4|Lifting. Her force is up, the bag moves up, so theta is zero. Work is m g h. Eight, times nine point eight, times one, about seventy eight joules.
C3|0.6|Carrying. Her hand still pushes up, but the bag moves sideways. Theta is ninety degrees, and cos ninety is zero. So her work on the bag is zero.
