P1|0.35|So what does it mean to resolve a vector?
P2|0.4|To resolve a vector means to split it into two vectors along chosen directions.
P3|0.4|These two parts are called components. Added together, they give back the original vector.
P4|0.6|Any two directions will do. But the easiest pair is the x axis and the y axis, at right angles.
P5|0.35|Think of shadows. A lamp straight overhead throws the arrow's shadow on the floor. That is the x component.
P6|0.65|A lamp at the side throws its shadow on the wall. That is the y component.

S7|0.35|Now let's slow it down past ninety degrees.
S8|0.65|The arrow leans left, so the x part points left. Its sign turns negative. A component can be negative.
D1|0.35|Now, why cosine and sine? One step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. The arrow and its two parts make a right triangle. A is the longest side.
D3|0.75|Step two. Cos theta is the side next to the angle, divided by the longest side. So A x equals A cos theta.
D4|0.75|Step three. Sin theta is the side across from the angle, divided by the longest side. So A y equals A sin theta.
D5|0.75|Step four. Going back. By Pythagoras, A equals the square root of A x squared plus A y squared.
D6|0.75|And tan theta equals A y divided by A x. That gives the direction.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Cosine goes with the side next to the angle. Sine goes with the side across from it.
C1|0.35|Let's compare two simple cases side by side.
C2|0.4|At zero degrees, cos is one and sin is zero. So A x is the full ten, and A y is zero.
C3|0.6|At ninety degrees, cos is zero and sin is one. So A x is zero, and A y is the full ten.
C4|3.2|Now you try. At forty five degrees, which part is bigger, A x or A y? Pause and decide.
C5|0.65|They are equal! Cos forty five and sin forty five are both about zero point seven oh seven. So each part is about seven point oh seven units.
K1|3.2|Here is one more. Can a component ever be longer than the vector itself? Pause and think.
K2|0.65|No! A component is a side of the right triangle, and the vector is the longest side. Cos and sin never go above one.
