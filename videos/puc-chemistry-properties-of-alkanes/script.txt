D1|0.35|Now the chemistry. Alkanes are lazy. But in light, chlorine can replace their hydrogens. This is halogenation. Let's follow it, link by link.
D2|0.75|Link one, initiation. Light splits a chlorine molecule evenly. Each chlorine atom keeps one electron. An atom with one unpaired electron is called a free radical.
D3|0.75|Link two. A chlorine radical pulls a hydrogen off methane. It makes hydrogen chloride, and leaves a methyl radical behind.
D4|0.75|Link three. The methyl radical grabs one chlorine atom from a chlorine molecule. Chloromethane forms, and a new chlorine radical is set free.
D5|0.75|That new radical starts link two again. One spark keeps a whole chain going. Links two and three are called propagation.
D6|0.75|Link four, termination. Two radicals meet and pair their electrons. Two methyl radicals can even make ethane. The chain stops.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Alkanes are lazy, but light, heat or a catalyst wakes them up.
