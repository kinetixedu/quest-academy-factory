H1|0.4|A soft potato. A gritty guava. A strong jute rope.
H2|0.45|All three come from plants. But one feels soft, one feels sandy, and one is strong enough to tie a cart.
H3|0.35|Diya bit into a guava and asked: what are these tiny hard grains, and why is a plant soft in one place and tough in another?
H4|0.5|The answer is tissues. A tissue is a team of cells that work together on one job.
H5|0.65|By the end, you will know the five permanent tissues of a plant, and spot each one by its cells.
D1|0.35|First, what does permanent mean here?
D2|0.45|Young cells at the tips keep dividing. When they stop dividing and take up one job for good, they become permanent tissue.
D3|0.45|A simple tissue is made of one type of cell: parenchyma, collenchyma and sclerenchyma.
D4|0.65|A complex tissue is made of many types of cells working as one: xylem and phloem.
Z1|0.35|Let's zoom into a young stem and meet the simple tissues.
Z2|0.5|Parenchyma is the soft filling. Its cells have thin walls of cellulose, often with small spaces between them.
Z3|0.5|Parenchyma makes food by photosynthesis, stores food, and secretes. The soft inside of a potato is mostly this.
Z4|0.5|Collenchyma sits just below the skin of young dicot stems. Its walls are thick only at the corners, with cellulose, hemicellulose and pectin.
Z5|0.5|That makes it strong but bendy, to hold up a young stem, or a leaf stalk, called the petiole.
Z6|0.65|Sclerenchyma is the tough one. Its walls are thick and hard with lignin, and its cells are usually dead. It comes as long fibres, and short sclereids, like the grains in a guava.
P1|0.35|Now the complex tissues. Watch them work inside the stem.
P2|0.75|Xylem carries water and minerals up from the roots. Its tracheids and vessels are dead, empty tubes with hard, lignified walls.
P3|0.75|A vessel is made of many cells joined end to end, like a pipe. Vessels are a feature of flowering plants. Gymnosperms lack them.
P4|0.75|Xylem also has xylem fibres for strength, and living xylem parenchyma that stores food.
P5|0.75|Phloem carries food, usually from the leaves to other parts. Food flows through sieve tubes. Their end walls have holes like a sieve: the sieve plates.
P6|0.75|A mature sieve tube has no nucleus. The companion cell beside it controls it. Phloem also has phloem parenchyma, and phloem fibres, like jute.
C1|0.35|Now the pair most students mix up: xylem and phloem.
C2|0.45|Both are complex tissues. Both are long tubes. Both run from root to leaf.
C3|0.65|But xylem carries water, and its tubes are dead. Phloem carries food, and its sieve tubes are alive.
C4|3.2|Your turn. A mature sieve tube has no nucleus. So who controls it: the companion cell, or the vessel? Pause and decide.
C5|0.65|The companion cell! It sits right beside the sieve tube, and its nucleus runs the sieve tube. A vessel is a dead xylem tube.
T1|0.6|Remember this. One type of cell makes a simple tissue. Many types of cells make a complex tissue.
W1|0.35|Now, a worked example. Let's match four clues to the right tissue.
W2|0.45|Walls thickened only at the corners, holding up a young stem. That is collenchyma.
W3|0.45|Hard grains in the pulp of a guava. That is sclerenchyma: the sclereids.
W4|0.45|Thin walls, living cells, food stored inside. That is parenchyma.
W5|0.65|End walls with sieve plates, carrying food. That is phloem.
X1|0.35|Now, the exam trap.
X2|0.4|Many students think every plant with xylem has vessels.
X3|0.65|But gymnosperms lack vessels. They move water through tracheids. Vessels are a feature of angiosperms.
