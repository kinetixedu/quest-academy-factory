H2|0.5|Two spoons? Two cups? Two kilograms? The number alone means nothing.
H3|0.65|It needs a unit. And that is the first big idea in physics: the physical quantity.
P1|0.35|So what is a physical quantity?
P2|0.35|It is anything in nature that we can measure. Length, mass, time, temperature, speed.
P3|0.6|Kindness and beauty matter, but they are not physical quantities. No instrument can measure them.
P4|0.35|Every measurement has two parts. A number, called the magnitude. And a unit.
P5|0.65|A unit is a fixed amount that everyone agrees to compare with, like one metre or one kilogram.
S1|0.35|Let's measure one pencil on our measuring bench.
S2|0.4|With a centimetre scale, it reads fifteen. Fifteen centimetres.
S3|0.4|Switch to millimetres. Now it reads one hundred and fifty.
S4|0.4|Switch to metres. Now it is only zero point one five.
S5|0.5|The pencil never changed. The number and the unit changed together.
S6|0.65|Look at the graph. Each time the unit gets ten times bigger, the number gets ten times smaller.
D1|0.35|Let's write this as a rule, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. A quantity Q equals its number n, times its unit u. Q equals n u.
D3|0.75|Step two. The same quantity in two different units does not change. So n one u one equals n two u two.
D4|0.75|Step three. The product n times u is fixed, so n is inversely proportional to u. A bigger unit means a smaller number.
D5|0.65|On the bench, the area of each box is n times u. The shape changes, but the area stays the same.
T1|0.7|Remember this. A physical quantity is always a number with a unit, never a number alone.
C1|0.35|Now, physicists sort quantities in two big ways.
C2|0.35|The first way: base quantities and derived quantities.
C3|0.35|Base quantities are the building blocks. The SI system has seven of them.
C4|0.4|Length, mass, time, electric current, temperature, amount of substance, and luminous intensity.
C5|0.35|Derived quantities are built from these. Area is length times length.
C6|0.65|Speed is length divided by time. So its unit is metre per second.
V1|0.35|The second way: scalars and vectors.
V2|0.35|A scalar has only a size. Mass, time and temperature are scalars.
V3|0.35|A vector has a size and a direction. Displacement, velocity and force are vectors.
V4|0.35|Push a box with ten newtons to the right. It moves right.
V5|0.65|Push it with the same ten newtons to the left. It moves left. Same size, different result. So force needs a direction.
G1|0.35|Let's sort six quantities on the board.
G2|0.35|Mass is a base quantity, and a scalar.
G3|0.35|Time is also base, and scalar.
G4|0.35|Speed and density are derived, and scalar.
G5|0.35|Velocity and force are derived, and they are vectors.
G6|0.65|Notice one box stays empty. None of the seven base quantities is a vector.
