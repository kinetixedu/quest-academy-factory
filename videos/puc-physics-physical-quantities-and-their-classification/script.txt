P1|0.35|So what is a physical quantity?
P2|0.35|It is anything in nature that we can measure. Length, mass, time, temperature, speed.
P3|0.6|Kindness and beauty matter, but no instrument can measure them. So they are not physical quantities.
P4|0.35|Every measurement has two parts. A number, called the magnitude. And a unit.
P5|0.65|A unit is a fixed amount we all agree on, like one metre or one kilogram.
S1|0.35|Let's measure one pencil on our measuring bench.
S2|0.4|With a centimetre scale, it reads fifteen. Fifteen centimetres.
S3|0.4|Switch to millimetres. Now it reads one hundred and fifty.
S4|0.4|Switch to metres. Now it is only zero point one five.
S5|0.5|The pencil never changed. The number and the unit changed together.
S6|0.65|Look at the graph. Unit ten times bigger, number ten times smaller.
D1|0.35|Let's write this as a rule, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. A quantity Q equals its number n, times its unit u. Q equals n u.
D3|0.75|Step two. The same quantity in two different units does not change. So n one u one equals n two u two.
D4|0.75|Step three. n times u is fixed, so n is inversely proportional to u. A bigger unit means a smaller number.
T1|0.7|Remember this. A physical quantity is always a number with a unit, never a number alone.
C1|0.35|Now, physicists sort quantities in two big ways.
C2|0.35|The first way: base quantities and derived quantities.
C3|0.35|Base quantities are the building blocks. The SI system picks seven of them.
C4|0.4|Length, mass, time, electric current, temperature, amount of substance, and luminous intensity.
R1|0.35|Derived quantities are built from these. Take speed.
R2|0.4|Speed equals distance divided by time.
R3|0.4|Distance is a length, so it is a base quantity. Time is also a base quantity.
R4|0.4|So speed is made from two base quantities. That is why speed is derived.
K1|0.4|Side by side. Length is base. You read it straight off a ruler.
K2|0.6|Speed is derived. You need a length and a time, and then you divide.
K3|3.2|Now you try. Is temperature a base quantity or a derived quantity? Pause the video and decide.
K4|0.65|Base! Temperature is one of the seven. A thermometer reads it directly, and it is not built from length, mass or time.
V1|0.35|The second way: scalars and vectors.
V2|0.35|A scalar has only a size. Five kilograms of rice is just five kilograms. No direction needed.
V3|0.4|A vector has a size and a direction. Displacement, velocity and force are vectors.
V4|0.35|Push a box with ten newtons to the right. It moves right.
V5|0.65|Push with the same ten newtons to the left, and it moves left. Same size, different result. So force needs a direction.

V7|0.65|A vector! Velocity is speed in a given direction. Fifty kilometres per hour north is not the same as fifty south.
