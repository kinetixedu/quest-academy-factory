I2|0.35|The coil that takes in the supply is the primary. The coil that gives the output is the secondary.
I3|0.65|The two coils never touch. They are linked only by magnetism. This is called mutual induction.
B1|0.35|Let's build one on the breadboard.
B2|0.35|An alternating current in the primary makes a magnetic flux in the core. The flux keeps changing.
B3|0.35|That changing flux passes through the secondary, and induces a voltage there.
B4|0.65|The output has the same frequency as the input. Only its size changes.
B5|0.35|Here the primary has five hundred turns, and the secondary has one hundred. Two hundred and thirty volts in, forty six volts out.

B7|0.65|Fewer turns on the secondary makes a step down transformer. More turns makes a step up transformer.
B8|0.35|Now try a battery. The flux builds up once, then stays still.
B9|0.65|A steady flux induces nothing. So the output is zero. A transformer works only on alternating current.
T1|0.8|No change in flux, no output.
D1|0.35|Now the transformer equation.
D2|0.7|Every turn of wire, on either coil, gets the same voltage from the changing flux.
D3|0.7|So the voltage across a coil is proportional to its number of turns.
D4|0.7|V s over V p equals N s over N p. This is called the turns ratio.
D5|0.7|In an ideal transformer, no energy is lost. Power in equals power out. So V p I p equals V s I s.
D6|0.65|That means I s over I p equals N p over N s. When the voltage steps up, the current steps down by the same factor.
W1|0.35|Worked example. A transformer has two thousand turns on the primary and one hundred on the secondary. The input is two hundred and thirty volts.

L1|0.35|Real transformers do lose a little energy.
L2|0.35|Copper loss, as heat in the coils. Eddy current loss, from small swirling currents in the core.
L3|0.35|That is why the core is made of thin insulated sheets, called laminations.

L5|0.65|Efficiency is output power divided by input power. Large transformers are very efficient, but never one hundred percent.
