M1|0.35|Now let's run the whole process, step by step.
M2|0.75|Step one. A spoon of old curd goes into warm milk. This starter carries millions of LAB.
M3|0.75|Step two. In the warm milk, the LAB feed and multiply. Each one divides again and again.
M4|0.75|Step three. As they grow, the LAB produce acids.
M5|0.75|Step four. The acids make the milk proteins coagulate, or clump, and partly digest them. The milk turns into curd.
M6|0.75|Step five. Curd is also richer in vitamin B12 than the milk was. And in our stomach, LAB help check harmful microbes.
U1|0.35|Curd is just the start. Let's see where else microbes work for us.
U2|0.45|At home. The batter for idli and dosa is fermented by bacteria. The carbon dioxide gas they make puffs it up. Bread dough rises with baker's yeast, Saccharomyces cerevisiae.
U3|0.45|In medicine and industry. Penicillin, an antibiotic, comes from the fungus Penicillium. Aspergillus niger, another fungus, makes citric acid.
U4|0.45|On farms. Rhizobium bacteria live in the root nodules of legumes and fix nitrogen from the air. Cyanobacteria like Nostoc and Anabaena fix nitrogen in special cells called heterocysts.
U5|0.65|For energy. Methanogens, like Methanobacterium, live in the gut of cattle. In cattle dung, they make biogas, mostly methane, also called gobar gas.
