H1|0.4|In week one, Aarav puts fifty rupees in a jar.
H2|0.45|Every week after that, he adds twenty rupees more. The same amount, every single week.
H3|0.35|He wants to know how much will be in the jar after one whole year. That is fifty two weeks.
H4|0.5|Does he have to add twenty, fifty one times, one week at a time?
H5|0.65|No. This pattern has a name, and a one line formula. It is called an arithmetic progression.
D1|0.4|Look at the jar each week. Fifty, seventy, ninety, one hundred ten.
D2|0.4|Each total is the one before it, plus the same twenty. Twenty is the step.
D3|0.65|A sequence like this is an arithmetic progression, or A P for short. Each term is the term before it, plus a fixed number.
D4|0.65|The first term is called a. The fixed step is called d, the common difference. Here, a is fifty and d is twenty.
L1|0.4|Now watch the weeks go by. n counts up, and each new bar is one step taller.
L2|0.4|Week two is fifty plus one step. Week three is fifty plus two steps. Week four, fifty plus three steps.
L3|0.65|See the pattern? The number of steps is always one less than the week number. Join the tops, and you get a straight line.
G1|0.35|Now let us build the formula in small steps.
G2|0.75|Step one. The first term is a. No steps yet.
G3|0.75|Step two. The second term is a plus d. The third is a plus two d.
G4|0.75|Step three. To reach term n, you take n minus one steps. So you add d, n minus one times.
G5|0.65|So the nth term is a n equals a, plus n minus one, times d. That is the general term of an A P.
S1|0.4|Side by side. When d is positive, the terms climb, like Aarav's savings. Fifty, seventy, ninety.
S2|0.6|When d is negative, the terms fall. Spending ten rupees a day from one hundred gives one hundred, ninety, eighty. Here d is minus ten.
S3|3.2|Now you try. In ten, seven, four, one, is d three, or minus three? Pause and decide.
S4|0.65|Minus three! d is always a term minus the term before it. Seven minus ten is minus three.
C1|0.4|How do you check if a sequence is an A P? Subtract each term from the next one. If every difference is the same, it is an A P.
C2|3.2|Quick one. Is three, six, twelve, twenty four an A P? Pause and think.
C3|0.65|No! The differences are three, six and twelve. They are not the same, so it is not an A P.
T1|0.7|Remember this. In an A P, add the same d every time. The nth term is a plus n minus one, times d.
W1|0.35|Now, Aarav's question. How much after fifty two weeks?
W2|0.75|Step one. a is fifty, d is twenty, and n is fifty two.
W3|0.75|Step two. a fifty two equals fifty, plus fifty one times twenty.
W4|0.75|Step three. Fifty one times twenty is one thousand and twenty.
W5|0.65|So after one year, the jar holds fifty plus one thousand and twenty. That is one thousand and seventy rupees.
