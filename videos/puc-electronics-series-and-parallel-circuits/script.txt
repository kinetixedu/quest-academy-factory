S3|0.65|In parallel, each part gets its own path, connected across the same two points. Like lanes on a road.
B1|0.35|Let's build both on the breadboard. A twelve volt supply, and two resistors, four ohms and twelve ohms.

B3|0.35|But the voltage is shared. Three volts across the four ohm resistor, nine volts across the twelve ohm resistor.
B4|0.65|Now break one resistor. The only path is cut. Everything stops. That is the festival string.
B5|0.35|Now rebuild them in parallel. Each resistor sits right across the twelve volt supply.
B6|0.35|Three amperes take the four ohm path. One ampere takes the twelve ohm path. The supply gives four amperes in total.
B7|0.65|Break one resistor now. Its path stops, but the other path keeps flowing. On the scope, the total drops, but never to zero.
T1|0.8|Series shares the voltage. Parallel shares the current.
D1|0.35|Now let's find the total resistance, starting with series.
D2|0.7|The supply voltage is shared. V equals V one plus V two.
D3|0.7|The current I is the same in both. So V equals I R one plus I R two.
D4|0.7|Divide by I. The series total is R one plus R two. Resistances simply add.
D5|0.35|Now parallel. The voltage V is the same across each one.
D6|0.7|The currents add. I equals I one plus I two.
D7|0.7|Each current is V over R. So V over R equals V over R one, plus V over R two.
D8|0.65|Divide by V. One over R equals one over R one, plus one over R two. The total is always smaller than the smallest resistor.

W3|0.35|In parallel, one over R is one sixth plus one third, which is one half. So R is two ohms.
W4|0.65|The supply then gives eighteen by two, nine amperes. Three through the six ohm resistor, and six through the three ohm resistor.
