R2|0.35|At the back sits the electron gun. A heated cathode gives off electrons.

R4|0.35|Anodes speed the electrons up and focus them into a thin beam.
R5|0.65|The beam hits a screen coated with phosphor, which glows as a bright spot.
R6|0.35|Two pairs of deflection plates steer the beam. The vertical plates move it up and down, with the input signal.
R7|0.35|The horizontal plates sweep it from left to right at a steady speed, then snap it back. That sawtooth comes from the time base.
R8|0.35|Put the two together, and the spot draws the waveform.
R9|0.65|A trigger circuit starts every sweep at the same point on the wave, so the picture stands still.

D2|0.35|Most use one beam and an electronic switch.

D4|0.65|In chop mode, it hops between the two many times during every sweep. This suits slow signals.
