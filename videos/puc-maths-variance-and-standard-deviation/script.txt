P1|0.4|Quick reminder. Last time, we measured spread with distances from the mean, using absolute values.
P2|0.65|Today there is another way to stop the minus signs from cancelling. Square every deviation. A square is never negative.
S1|0.4|Watch it on eight small numbers: two, four, four, four, five, five, seven and nine. Their mean is five.
S2|0.4|Each gap from five becomes the side of a real square. A gap of three makes a square of nine. A gap of four makes a square of sixteen.
S3|0.4|Now add all the square areas. Nine, one, one, one, zero, zero, four and sixteen. The total is thirty two.
S4|0.4|Share it among the eight numbers. Thirty two over eight is four. That is the variance: the average square.
S5|0.65|Now take the side of that average square. The square root of four is two. That is the standard deviation.
D1|0.4|Now the rule, step by step.
D2|0.75|Step one. Find the mean, x bar.
D3|0.75|Step two. Find each deviation, x minus x bar, and square it.
D4|0.75|Step three. Add the squares, and divide by n. This is the variance, written sigma squared.
D5|0.75|Step four. Take the positive square root. This is the standard deviation, sigma.
D6|0.65|A useful shortcut. The variance is also the mean of the squares, minus the square of the mean.
C1|0.4|Side by side. The variance squares the gaps, so its unit is squared too. For runs, that is runs squared, which is hard to picture.
C2|0.6|The standard deviation takes the square root, so it is back in runs. Batsman A has about thirty six. Batsman B has about four point two.
