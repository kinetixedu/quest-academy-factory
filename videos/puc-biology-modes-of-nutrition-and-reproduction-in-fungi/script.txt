R1|0.35|Now, how does a fungus make more of itself? Three routes.
R2|0.45|Route one is vegetative: fragmentation, fission or budding. A broken piece of hypha simply grows into a new mycelium.
R3|0.45|Route two is asexual spores. One parent makes spores called conidia, sporangiospores or zoospores. Each spore is a copy of the parent.
R4|0.65|Route three is sexual. Two parents join, and the spores are oospores, ascospores or basidiospores, made in fruiting bodies.
S1|0.35|The sexual route always runs in this order.
S2|0.75|Step one. Two hyphae of compatible mating types meet and join. Each is haploid: it has one set of chromosomes, written n.
S3|0.75|Step two. Plasmogamy. Their protoplasm fuses, so one cell now holds two separate nuclei. We write this as n plus n.
S4|0.75|Step three. In ascomycetes and basidiomycetes, this two-nuclei stage lasts for a while. It is called a dikaryon.
S5|0.75|Step four. Karyogamy. The two nuclei finally fuse. The cell becomes diploid, with two sets of chromosomes, written 2n.
S6|0.75|Step five. Meiosis. The diploid nucleus divides and halves the chromosomes, giving haploid spores.
C1|0.35|Let's put asexual and sexual spores side by side.
C2|0.45|Asexual spores come from one parent, without any fusion. Example: the sporangiospores of the bread mould.
C3|0.65|Sexual spores come only after plasmogamy, karyogamy and meiosis, and mix features of two parents. Example: a mushroom's basidiospores.

C5|0.65|Sexual! Meiosis comes only after two nuclei have fused.
T1|0.6|Remember this. Plasmogamy first, karyogamy second, meiosis last.
W1|0.35|Now a worked example: the sexual cycle of a mushroom, Agaricus.
W2|0.45|With no sex organs, plasmogamy happens when two vegetative cells of different strains fuse.
