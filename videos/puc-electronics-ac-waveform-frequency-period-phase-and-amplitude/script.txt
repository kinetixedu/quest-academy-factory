I1|0.35|With four ideas. Amplitude, period, frequency and phase.
I2|0.65|Let's build them one at a time, on the scope.
G1|0.35|On the breadboard, an A C source drives a resistor. Watch the electrons. They do not flow round. They rock back and forth.
G2|0.35|Inside the source, picture an arm turning in a circle at a steady speed. Its height at each moment is the voltage.
G3|0.65|As the arm turns, the scope draws its height. That smooth shape is a sine wave.
AM1|0.35|First, amplitude. It is the highest value the wave reaches, its peak. We write it V m.
AM2|0.65|Turn up the amplitude, and the wave grows taller. The arm simply gets longer.
P1|0.35|Next, the period, T. It is the time for one complete cycle, one full turn of the arm.
P2|0.35|And the frequency, f, is how many cycles happen in one second. It is measured in hertz.
P3|0.65|So f equals one over T. Turn the frequency up, and the waves squeeze closer together. The electrons rock faster too.
D1|0.35|Now let's write the wave as an equation.
D2|0.7|One full turn is three hundred and sixty degrees, or two pi radians.
D3|0.7|The arm makes f turns every second. So its angular speed is omega equals two pi f, in radians per second.
D4|0.7|After a time t, the arm has turned through an angle omega t. Its height is V m times the sine of omega t.
D5|0.65|So small v equals V m sine omega t. Small v is the value at any instant.
PH1|0.35|The last idea is phase. Phase tells you where in its cycle a wave is, at a given moment.
PH2|0.35|Now put a second wave on the scope, with the same frequency, but a quarter turn ahead.
PH3|0.35|Its peaks come first. We say it leads by ninety degrees. That gap is the phase difference.
PH4|0.65|We write it as v equals V m sine of omega t plus phi. Phi is the phase angle.
T1|0.8|Amplitude says how big. Frequency says how often. Phase says when.
