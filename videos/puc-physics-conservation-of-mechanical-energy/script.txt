P1|0.35|So what is mechanical energy?
P2|0.4|It has two parts. Kinetic energy, K, is the energy of motion.
P3|0.4|Potential energy, U, is energy stored by height, or in a stretched spring.
P4|0.4|Add them, and you get the mechanical energy, E. E equals K plus U.
P5|0.65|The rule: if only conservative forces like gravity do work, E never changes. K and U trade places.
S1|0.35|Let's run it. A twenty kilogram child on a swing, two point five metres long, pulled back to sixty degrees.
S2|0.4|At the top, the seat is one point two five metres up. U is two hundred and forty five joules. K is zero.
S3|0.4|Let go. As it swings down, U falls and K rises.
S4|0.4|At the bottom, U is zero, and K is the full two hundred and forty five joules. Speed: about five metres per second.
S5|0.5|Up the other side, K turns back into U. It stops at the same height it started from.
S6|0.65|On the graph, U and K rise and fall, but their sum, E, is a flat line.
S7|0.35|Slow-mo replay, near the bottom.
S8|0.65|The rope pulls at right angles to the motion, so it does no work. Only gravity changes the speed.
D1|0.35|Now the proof, step by step.
D2|0.75|Step one. The work energy theorem. The work done on a body equals its change in kinetic energy.
D3|0.75|Step two. For a conservative force, the work equals minus the change in potential energy.
D4|0.75|Step three. So delta K equals minus delta U. What K gains, U loses.
D5|0.75|Step four. Delta K plus delta U equals zero. So K plus U never changes.
D6|0.75|Step five. Between any two points, K one plus U one equals K two plus U two.
T1|0.7|Remember this. With only conservative forces at work, K plus U stays the same.
R1|0.35|So how fast is the seat at the bottom? Follow the links.
R2|0.4|One. At the top, it is at rest. All its energy is U, m g h.
R3|0.4|Two. At the bottom, h is zero. All its energy is K, half m v squared.
R4|0.4|Three. So half m v squared equals m g h. The mass cancels.
R5|0.6|Four. v equals root two g h. Root of two times nine point eight times one point two five. About four point nine five metres per second.
