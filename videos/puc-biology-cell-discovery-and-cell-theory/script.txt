B1|0.35|First, what is a cell?
B2|0.45|Think of bricks in a wall. One brick is small, but a whole building is made of bricks.
B3|0.65|A cell is like that brick. Some living things are just one cell. They are unicellular, and they can live on their own. Anything less than a whole cell cannot live by itself. So the cell is the basic unit of structure and function of life.
Z1|0.35|Let's zoom into an onion peel, just like the early scientists did.
Z2|0.5|At low zoom, the peel is a sheet of tiny boxes, packed side by side. Each box is one cell.
Z3|0.5|Zoom into one cell. Its outer boundary is a firm cell wall. Just inside it is the cell membrane. The jelly that fills the cell is the cytoplasm.
Z4|0.5|Zoom again. Inside sits a dense, round body wrapped in a membrane. This is the nucleus. It holds chromosomes, which carry the genetic material, DNA.
Z5|0.65|Cells with a membrane bound nucleus like this are called eukaryotic. Cells without one are prokaryotic.
L1|0.35|Now the story of the cell theory, step by step.
L2|0.75|Step one. Anton Von Leeuwenhoek first saw and described a live cell. Later, Robert Brown discovered the nucleus.
L3|0.75|Step two. In 1838, Matthias Schleiden, a German botanist, studied many plants. He found that all plants are made of different kinds of cells, which form the tissues of the plant.
L4|0.75|Step three. In 1839, Theodore Schwann studied animal cells. He saw a thin outer layer, which we now call the plasma membrane. He also found that a cell wall is special to plant cells.
L5|0.75|Step four. Schwann said that the bodies of animals and plants are made of cells and products of cells. Together, Schleiden and Schwann gave the cell theory. But it did not explain how new cells are formed.
L6|0.75|Step five. In 1855, Rudolf Virchow explained that cells divide, and new cells form from cells that already exist. In Latin, omnis cellula e cellula. He gave the cell theory its final shape.
T1|0.6|Remember this. Every cell comes from a cell.
C1|0.35|So, the cell theory as we know it today has just two rules.
C2|0.45|Rule one. All living organisms are made of cells and products of cells.
C3|0.45|Products of cells are things that cells make and leave around them, like the wall around a plant cell.
C4|0.65|Rule two. All cells arise from pre existing cells, cells that were already there. Watch one cell divide. Now there are two.
D1|0.35|Cells come in very different sizes.
D2|0.45|The smallest cells are Mycoplasmas, only zero point three micrometres long. A micrometre is one thousandth of a millimetre.
D3|0.45|Bacteria can be three to five micrometres long. A human red blood cell is about seven micrometres across.
D4|0.65|The largest single cell is the egg of an ostrich. And nerve cells are some of the longest cells.
U1|0.35|Cells also come in many shapes, and each shape suits its job.
U2|0.45|A red blood cell is round and biconcave, pressed in on both sides. A white blood cell is amoeboid. It keeps changing its shape.
U3|0.6|A nerve cell is long and branched. Columnar epithelial cells are long and narrow. In plants, a tracheid is long, and mesophyll cells are round or oval.
