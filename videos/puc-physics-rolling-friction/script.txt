P1|0.35|So what is rolling friction?
P2|0.4|When a body rolls over a surface, the force that opposes the rolling is rolling friction.
P3|0.4|A perfectly hard wheel on a perfectly hard floor touches at one point, and that point does not slide. No friction at all.
P5|0.65|But real wheels and floors squash a little where they meet. That squashing causes rolling friction.

D1|0.35|Now the rule, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. On a level floor, the normal force N equals m g.
D3|0.75|Step two. Sliding friction. f k equals mu k times N.
D4|0.75|Step three. Rolling friction has the same form: f r equals mu r times N. Mu r has no unit.
D5|0.75|Step four. Same load, same N. So f r over f k equals mu r over mu k.
D6|0.75|Step five. Mu r is far smaller than mu k. So rolling friction is far smaller.
T1|0.7|Remember this. For the same load, rolling friction is much smaller than sliding friction.
