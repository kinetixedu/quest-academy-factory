P1|0.35|Last time we learnt, delta U equals q plus w.
P2|0.4|In a sealed steel box, the volume cannot change. So the gas does no work, and all the heat goes into U.
P3|0.4|But in a kitchen, the pressure stays steady and the volume can change.
P4|0.4|A gas that forms must push the air away, like making room in a crowded bus. That push costs energy.
P5|0.4|So chemists use a new state function, enthalpy, H. H equals U plus p V.
P6|0.65|At constant pressure, the heat taken in equals delta H.
S1|0.35|Now the link between delta H and delta U, step by step.
S2|0.75|At constant pressure, delta H equals delta U plus p delta V.
S3|0.75|For gases, p V equals n R T. So p delta V equals delta n g R T.
S4|0.75|Delta n g is the moles of gas in the products, minus the moles of gas in the reactants.
S5|0.75|So delta H equals delta U plus delta n g R T.
S6|0.65|If delta H is minus, heat comes out. That is exothermic. If delta H is plus, heat goes in. That is endothermic.
V1|0.35|Now, back to the pan and the water, on our molecule bench.
V2|0.4|Heat capacity, C, is the heat needed to warm something by one degree. q equals C times delta T.
V3|0.4|A big heat capacity is like a wide bucket. Pour in lots, and the level rises only a little.
V4|0.4|Specific heat capacity is the heat for one gram, by one kelvin. For water, about four point one eight joules.
V5|0.4|So q equals m c delta T. Mass, times specific heat, times the rise.
V6|0.4|To warm one hundred grams of water by ten kelvin, you need four thousand one hundred eighty joules.
V7|0.65|Steel needs far less heat per gram. So the pan races ahead, and the water lags behind. Sand and sea work the same way.
C1|0.35|For one mole of an ideal gas, there are two heat capacities.
C2|0.4|C v, at constant volume. All the heat warms the gas.
C3|0.4|C p, at constant pressure. Some heat is spent pushing the piston up. So C p is bigger.
C4|0.65|The gap is exactly R. C p minus C v equals R.
K1|0.4|We measure these heats with a calorimeter. A bomb calorimeter is a sealed steel vessel in water. Its volume is fixed.
K2|0.4|A coffee cup calorimeter is open to the air, at steady pressure.
K3|0.4|In both, we read how much the water warms, and use q equals C delta T.
