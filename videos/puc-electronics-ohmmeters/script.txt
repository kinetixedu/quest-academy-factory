O1|0.35|An ohmmeter measures resistance. But a resistor cannot push any current by itself.
O2|0.35|So the ohmmeter carries its own battery inside.
O3|0.65|The simplest one is the series ohmmeter. A cell, a galvanometer and a resistor, all in series, ending in two probes.
B1|0.35|Let's run it on the breadboard.
B2|0.35|First, short the probes together. Turn the zero adjust knob until the needle sits exactly at full scale.
B3|0.65|That mark is zero ohms. The current is as big as it can be.
B4|0.65|Now pull the probes apart. No current flows, so the needle falls back to the left. That mark is infinity.
B5|0.35|Put an unknown resistor between the probes. Less current flows, and the needle stops somewhere in between.
B6|0.65|Watch the scope. A bigger resistance gives a smaller current. That is why the scale runs backwards.
D1|0.35|So where does each mark go? Let's work it out.
D2|0.7|Call the ohmmeter's own total resistance R in. With the probes shorted, the full scale current is E divided by R in.
D3|0.7|With an unknown resistor R x, the current is E divided by R in plus R x.
D4|0.7|Divide the two. The needle's fraction of full scale is R in, divided by R in plus R x.
D5|0.7|When R x equals R in, the needle stops exactly at half scale.
D6|0.65|And doubling R x does not halve the needle. So the marks crowd together on the left.
W1|0.35|Worked example. An ohmmeter has a one point five volt cell and a meter that reads one milliampere at full scale.
W2|0.6|R in equals one point five divided by one times ten to the minus three. That is fifteen hundred ohms.
W3|0.65|So the half scale mark reads fifteen hundred ohms.
W4|0.6|Now connect three thousand ohms. The fraction is fifteen hundred divided by four thousand five hundred.
W5|0.65|That is one third of full scale.
Z1|0.35|Why do we need the zero adjust knob at all?
Z2|0.65|As the cell gets weaker, E falls, and the needle no longer reaches full scale. The knob brings it back, so zero ohms stays at zero.
S1|0.35|For very small resistances, a shunt type ohmmeter puts the resistor in parallel with the meter instead.
S2|0.65|Its scale runs the normal way, with zero ohms on the left.
P1|0.35|Two rules keep the reading honest.
P2|0.35|Never measure resistance in a circuit that is switched on. Its own voltage spoils the reading, and can damage the meter.
P3|0.65|And take the part out, or lift one end, so other parts connected across it do not change the reading.
T1|0.8|An ohmmeter does not measure ohms directly. It measures current, and turns it into ohms.
X1|0.35|Now, the exam trap.
X2|0.35|Many students think the ohmmeter scale is even, like an ammeter's.
X3|0.65|It is not. The scale is reversed and crowded. Half scale means R x equals R in, not half of some range.
