P2|0.4|When a rope is pulled tight, it pulls on whatever is tied to it. That pull is the tension, T.
P3|0.4|Tension always points along the rope, away from the body. A rope can pull, but it can never push.
P4|0.4|For a light rope, with almost no mass, the tension is the same all along it.

D1|0.35|Now let's write it down, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. Two forces act on the bucket. T pulls up, and m g pulls down.
D3|0.75|Step two. Take up as positive. The net force is T minus m g. By the second law, that equals m a.
D4|0.75|Step three. Move m g across. T equals m g plus m a. That is, T equals m times g plus a.
D5|0.75|Step four. Hanging still, or moving at a steady speed, a is zero. So T equals m g.
D6|0.75|Step five. If a points down, a is negative. Then T equals m times g minus a.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Tension follows the acceleration, not the direction of motion.
