B1|0.35|First, what is respiration?
B2|0.45|Food holds energy in the bonds between its carbon atoms. Breaking these bonds by oxidation inside cells releases energy. This is cellular respiration.
B3|0.65|The energy is not used all at once. It is trapped in ATP, the energy currency of the cell. Glucose and oxygen give carbon dioxide, water and energy.
Z1|0.35|So, do plants breathe? Let's zoom in.
Z2|0.5|A plant has no lungs. Each part, the roots, stem and leaves, takes care of its own gas exchange.
Z3|0.5|Zoom into a leaf. Its cells are loosely packed, with air spaces between them. Almost every living cell touches air.

L1|0.35|Now glycolysis. Follow one glucose in the cytoplasm, step by step.
L2|0.75|Step one. Glucose, a six carbon sugar, takes a phosphate from ATP. The enzyme is hexokinase. It becomes glucose six phosphate.
L3|0.75|Step two. It is rearranged to fructose six phosphate. A second ATP adds one more phosphate. Now it is fructose one six bisphosphate.
L4|0.75|Step three. This sugar splits into two three carbon molecules, PGAL and DHAP. DHAP changes into PGAL too, so we follow two PGAL.
L5|0.75|Step four. Each PGAL gives two hydrogen atoms to NAD plus, making NADH. It becomes BPGA. Then BPGA becomes PGA and makes one ATP.
L6|0.75|Step five. A few steps later, PEP becomes pyruvic acid and makes one more ATP. One glucose gives two pyruvic acid.
T1|0.6|Remember this. One glucose gives two pyruvic acid.
C1|0.35|Now count the ATP.
C2|0.45|Two ATP are used at the start, one at each phosphate step.
C3|0.65|Each PGAL makes two ATP, and there are two PGAL. So four ATP are made. Four made, minus two used, leaves a net gain of two ATP, plus two NADH.
D1|0.35|Who runs glycolysis, and where?
D2|0.45|It happens in the cytoplasm of all living organisms. In organisms that live without oxygen, it is the only way to respire.
D3|0.65|It is also called the EMP pathway, after Gustav Embden, Otto Meyerhof and J. Parnas. Plants get glucose from sucrose. The enzyme invertase splits sucrose into glucose and fructose.
U1|0.35|What happens to pyruvic acid next? It has three roads.
U2|0.45|Without oxygen, it can become lactic acid, as in our muscles, or ethanol and carbon dioxide, as in yeast. This is fermentation.
U3|0.6|With oxygen, it enters the mitochondria and is broken down fully to carbon dioxide and water. This is aerobic respiration.
U4|3.2|Your turn. Does glycolysis itself need oxygen? Pause and decide.
U5|0.65|No, it does not! That is why even organisms living without oxygen can run it.
W1|0.35|Now, a worked example. A cell breaks down three glucose by glycolysis. Count the products.

W3|0.45|Each glucose gives a net two ATP. So three give six ATP.
W4|0.65|Each glucose gives two NADH. So three give six NADH. Six, six and six.
