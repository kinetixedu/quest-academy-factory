D1|0.35|Now, why does the slope give the acceleration? Step by step.
D2|0.75|Step one. Acceleration is the change in velocity, divided by the time taken.
D3|0.75|Step two. On the graph, the change in velocity is the rise, and the time taken is the run.
D4|0.75|Step three. Slope is rise over run. So the slope is the acceleration.
D5|0.7|For Aarav, six over four is one point five metres per second squared. When braking, minus six over three is minus two.
R1|0.35|Now the area under the line. Here is the big idea.
R2|0.75|At a steady velocity, displacement is velocity times time. On the graph, that is height times width. The area of a rectangle.
R3|0.75|The units agree too. Metres per second, times seconds, gives metres.
R4|0.7|So for the whole ride, the first triangle gives twelve metres, the rectangle twenty four, and the last triangle nine.
R5|0.65|Forty five metres in total. The meter never showed it. The graph did.
T1|0.7|Remember this. The slope of a velocity-time graph is the acceleration. The area under it is the displacement.
