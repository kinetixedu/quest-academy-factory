B1|0.35|First, the cell wall.
B2|0.45|The cell wall is a non living, rigid layer outside the plasma membrane. It is found in plants and fungi.
B3|0.65|It gives the cell its shape, and protects it from damage and infection. It helps cells interact, and it keeps out large unwanted molecules. In algae, the wall is made of cellulose, galactans, mannans and minerals like calcium carbonate. In other plants, it is cellulose, hemicellulose, pectins and proteins.
Z1|0.35|Let's zoom into the walls of plant cells.
Z2|0.5|Here are neighbouring plant cells, side by side. Between them is a layer called the middle lamella. It is mainly calcium pectate, and it glues the cells together.
Z3|0.5|Zoom in. A young cell has a primary wall, which can grow. As the cell matures, a secondary wall forms on the inner side, towards the membrane.
Z4|0.5|Zoom again. Tiny channels cross the wall and the middle lamella. These are plasmodesmata.
Z5|0.65|Plasmodesmata connect the cytoplasm of neighbouring cells, so the cells can share materials.
L1|0.35|Now inside the cell, the endomembrane system, step by step.
L2|0.75|Step one. The endomembrane system is a group of membrane bound organelles whose work is coordinated. It includes the endoplasmic reticulum, the Golgi complex, lysosomes and vacuoles.
L3|0.75|Step two. Mitochondria, chloroplasts and peroxisomes are not part of it, because their work is not coordinated with these.
L4|0.75|Step three. The endoplasmic reticulum, or ER, is a network of tiny tubes. It splits the inside of the cell into two spaces: the lumen inside the ER, and the cytoplasm outside it.
L5|0.75|Step four. ER with ribosomes on its surface is rough ER. It is common in cells that make and secrete proteins, and it joins the outer membrane of the nucleus.
L6|0.75|Step five. ER without ribosomes is smooth ER. It is the main site for making lipids. In animal cells, it makes steroid hormones.
T1|0.6|Remember this. ER makes, Golgi packs, vesicles ship.
C1|0.35|Now the Golgi apparatus.
C2|0.45|Camillo Golgi first saw it in 1898, as darkly stained structures near the nucleus. It is a stack of flat, disc shaped sacs, called cisternae, each about zero point five to one micrometre across.
C3|0.45|The stack has two faces. The cis face, or forming face, is convex. The trans face, or maturing face, is concave.
C4|0.65|Vesicles from the ER join the cis face, and their contents move towards the trans face. Along the way, proteins are changed. The Golgi is an important site for making glycoproteins and glycolipids.
D1|0.35|Two more members of the system: lysosomes and vacuoles.
D2|0.45|Lysosomes are small bags formed by packing in the Golgi apparatus. They are full of digestive enzymes, called hydrolases, that work best in acid.
D3|0.45|These enzymes, lipases, proteases and carbohydrases, can digest carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids.
D4|0.65|A vacuole is a space bound by a single membrane, the tonoplast. It holds water, sap and waste. In plant cells, vacuoles can fill up to ninety per cent of the cell.
U1|0.35|Vacuoles do more than store.
U2|0.45|In plants, the tonoplast moves ions into the vacuole against the concentration gradient. So their concentration inside the vacuole is much higher than in the cytoplasm.
U3|0.6|In Amoeba, a contractile vacuole removes extra water and waste. And in protists, food vacuoles form when the cell swallows food particles.
