D1|0.45|First, where does a root come from? Inside every seed sits a tiny baby root, called the radicle.
D2|0.6|In mustard, the radicle keeps growing into a main root, the primary root. Side roots branch out from it. Together they make a tap root system.
D3|0.6|In wheat, the primary root lives only a short time. Many thin roots grow from the base of the stem instead. This is a fibrous root system.
D4|0.6|In grass, Monstera and the banyan tree, roots grow from parts other than the radicle. These are called adventitious roots.
D5|0.65|Roots do four jobs: they absorb water and minerals, hold the plant firmly, store food, and make plant growth regulators.
Z1|0.35|Now let's zoom into the tip of a root, where all the growing happens.
Z2|0.6|At the very end sits the root cap, shaped like a thimble, the small cap a tailor wears on one finger. It protects the soft tip as it pushes through the soil.
Z3|0.75|Just above it is the region of meristematic activity. Its cells are very small, with thin walls and dense protoplasm, and they divide again and again.
Z4|0.75|Above that is the region of elongation. Here the new cells stretch and grow longer, and this is how the root grows in length.
Z5|0.75|Then comes the region of maturation. The cells settle into their final jobs, and some skin cells grow fine threads called root hairs.
Z6|0.65|Root hairs are the real drinkers. They absorb water and minerals from the soil.
P1|0.5|Now the big question. Roots also change shape for three other jobs: storing food, support, and breathing.
P2|0.75|Job one, storing food. The tap roots of carrot and turnip swell up with stored food.
P3|0.75|Sweet potato stores food too. But its swollen roots are adventitious roots, not a tap root.
P4|0.75|Job two, support. A banyan tree sends roots down from its branches. They reach the soil and hold up the heavy branches like pillars. These are prop roots.
P5|0.75|In maize and sugarcane, supporting roots come out of the lower nodes of the stem. A node is a point on the stem where a leaf grows. These are stilt roots.
P6|0.75|Job three, breathing. Rhizophora grows in swampy mud, where there is very little air. Many of its roots come out of the ground and grow straight up.
P7|0.75|These are pneumatophores. They help the plant get oxygen for respiration.
