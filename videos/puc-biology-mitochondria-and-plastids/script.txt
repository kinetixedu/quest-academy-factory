D1|0.35|First, two new words.
D2|0.45|An organelle is a tiny part inside a cell with its own job, just like an organ inside your body.
D3|0.45|Mitochondria and plastids are two special organelles. Each one is wrapped in two membranes, not one.
D4|0.65|A membrane is a thin skin, like the skin of a soap bubble. Two skins, one inside the other, make a double membrane.
Z1|0.35|Let's zoom into a cell, one level at a time.
Z2|0.5|A cell holds many mitochondria. They are shaped like sausages or cylinders. A busy cell has more of them, a resting cell has fewer.
Z3|0.5|Zoom into one mitochondrion. It has an outer membrane, and an inner membrane. Between them, the inside is split into two watery rooms.
Z4|0.5|The inner membrane folds inward, again and again. These folds are called cristae. Folding gives much more surface area, more room to work.
Z5|0.65|Zoom into the matrix, the inner room. It holds a single circular DNA molecule, a few RNA molecules, and small ribosomes, called 70S ribosomes. Mitochondria divide by splitting in two, called fission.
P1|0.35|Now watch what a mitochondrion does, one step at a time.
P2|0.75|Step one. Food, like sugar, reaches the mitochondrion.
P3|0.75|Step two. Oxygen comes in too. Using oxygen to release energy is called aerobic respiration.
P4|0.75|Step three. The food is broken down, step by step, along the cristae.
P5|0.75|Step four. The energy is packed into small molecules called ATP. Think of ATP as charged batteries.
P6|0.75|Step five. ATP travels out, and powers the whole cell. That is why mitochondria are called the power houses of the cell.
C1|0.35|Now the plastids. They are found in all plant cells, and in euglenoids.
C2|0.5|Plastids are named by the pigment they carry. A pigment is a coloured chemical. Chloroplasts carry chlorophyll and carotenoids. They trap light energy for photosynthesis, the way a plant makes its food.
C3|0.5|Inside a chloroplast, the space is called the stroma. Flat sacs called thylakoids are stacked like piles of coins. Each stack is a granum. Flat tubes called stroma lamellae join the stacks.
C4|0.5|A green alga called Chlamydomonas has just one chloroplast per cell. A leaf mesophyll cell can have twenty to forty.
C5|0.5|Chromoplasts carry carotenoid pigments, like carotene. They give yellow, orange and red colours. That is Diya's tomato. As it ripens, its chloroplasts turn into chromoplasts, and the green gives way to red.
C6|0.65|Leucoplasts have no colour. They store food. Amyloplasts store starch, like in a potato. Elaioplasts store oils and fats. Aleuroplasts store proteins.
V1|0.75|Side by side. Where. Mitochondria are found in almost all eukaryotic cells, plant and animal. Chloroplasts are found in plant cells and euglenoids.
V2|0.75|The inner membrane. In a mitochondrion it folds into cristae. In a chloroplast it wraps the stroma, which holds the thylakoids.
V3|0.75|The job. Mitochondria release energy from food, as ATP. Chloroplasts trap light energy to make food.
