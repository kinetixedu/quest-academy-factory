Z1|0.35|Let's zoom into the membrane.
Z2|0.5|Here is a whole cell. The membrane is the thin line all around it.
Z3|0.5|Zoom into the edge. The membrane is two layers of lipids, a lipid bilayer. The main lipids are phospholipids.
Z4|0.5|Zoom again, into one phospholipid. It has a polar head, which loves water, and two tails, which keep away from water. The tails are hydrophobic, meaning water fearing.
Z5|0.65|So in the bilayer, the heads face the water on both sides, and the tails hide in the middle, safe from water. Just like the oil drop, the tails keep away from water.
L1|0.35|Now let's build the full membrane, step by step.
L2|0.75|Step one. Two layers of phospholipids, heads out and tails in.
L3|0.75|Step two. Add cholesterol, which also sits inside the membrane.
L4|0.75|Step three. Add integral proteins. These are partly or totally buried in the membrane.
L5|0.75|Step four. Add peripheral proteins. These lie on the surface of the membrane.
L6|0.75|Step five. Add carbohydrates. The membrane has proteins and carbohydrates too, and the amount of protein and lipid changes from one cell type to another.
T1|0.6|Remember this. Fluid, because the parts move sideways. Mosaic, because many pieces fit together.
C1|0.35|This picture is called the fluid mosaic model. It was proposed by Singer and Nicolson, in 1972.
C2|0.45|The lipids behave almost like a liquid. So the proteins can move sideways within the bilayer, like boats drifting on a pond. This ability to move is the membrane's fluidity.
C3|0.45|Fluidity is important. It helps in cell growth, in forming joins between cells, and in secretion.
C4|0.65|It also helps in endocytosis, when the cell takes things in by folding its membrane, and in cell division.
D1|0.35|The membrane's most important job is transport. Moving molecules across it.
D2|0.45|Passive transport needs no energy. Neutral molecules move by simple diffusion, from higher concentration to lower, like a crowd spreading out.
D3|0.45|Water also moves from higher to lower concentration across the membrane. This is called osmosis.
D4|0.65|Polar molecules cannot pass through the oily middle of the bilayer. They need a carrier protein in the membrane to help them across.
U1|0.35|Some molecules must go the other way, from lower to higher concentration. Against the gradient.
U2|0.45|This needs energy, from ATP, the cell's energy currency. It is called active transport.
U3|0.6|An example is the sodium potassium pump. It pumps sodium ions out and potassium ions in, using ATP.
