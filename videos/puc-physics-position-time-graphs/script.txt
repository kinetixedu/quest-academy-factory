P1|0.35|So what is a position-time graph?
P2|0.4|We pick a starting point, called the origin. Position x is how far you are from it. Ahead is positive.
P3|0.4|Time goes on the horizontal axis. Position goes on the vertical axis.
P4|0.6|At every moment, we mark one dot: where you are, at that time. Join the dots, and you get the x-t graph.
S1|0.35|Let's ride with Aarav on our motion notebook.
S2|0.4|Part one. He rides at a steady five metres per second. Every second, he moves the same five metres.
S3|0.5|Equal steps in equal times. So the graph is a straight sloping line.
S4|0.4|Part two. He stops at a signal. Time keeps going, but the position stays at fifty metres.
S5|0.5|The graph goes flat. A flat line means the object is at rest.
S6|0.4|Part three. He rides faster, ten metres per second. Now the line is steeper.
S7|0.6|Steeper line, faster motion. The steepness of the line tells you the speed.
D1|0.35|Let's turn steepness into a number, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. Pick two points on the line. Read their times and their positions.
D3|0.75|Step two. The change in position, delta x, is x two minus x one. The change in time, delta t, is t two minus t one.
D4|0.75|Step three. Slope equals delta x over delta t. That is rise over run.
D5|0.75|Step four. Delta x over delta t is exactly the velocity. So the slope of an x-t graph is the velocity.
T1|0.7|Remember this. On a position-time graph, the slope is the velocity.
K1|0.4|Side by side. One line goes up. The position grows, so the velocity is positive.
K2|0.6|The other line goes down. The position shrinks. The object is coming back towards the origin, so the velocity is negative.
K3|3.2|Now you try. A line slopes down. Is the object moving away from the origin, or towards it? Pause and decide.
K4|0.65|Towards it! A falling line means x is getting smaller, so the object moves back towards the origin. Its velocity is negative.
