## Quiz
Q: A 75 W fan runs for 10 hours. How many units does it use?
- A) 7.5 units
- B) 0.75 units (correct)
- C) 75 units
- D) 750 units
Correct: B. Takeaway strip: "Units = kW x hours."

## Recap rows
- 1 unit = 1 kWh - chip `kW x h`
- Bill = units x rate - chip `+ FIXED`
- Battery = V x Ah - chip `USE HALF`

## Facts and sources
- 1 kWh = 3.6 MJ; in India the kWh is called a "unit": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour
- Deep discharge shortens lead-acid life; stationary/solar batteries designed for about 20-50% depth of discharge: https://rameshnatarajankailad.substack.com/p/deep-discharge-of-lead-acid-batteries
- Lead-acid electrolyte is sulphuric acid and charging gives off hydrogen (flammable) - standard battery safety.
- Time-of-day tariffs (cheaper in solar hours for commercial/industrial users, Ministry of Power 2023 rules): https://www.dtnext.in/news/business/new-tariff-rule-to-allow-20-saving-in-bills-for-usage-during-solar-hours-720014 (K2 bakery line says only "when its tariff is cheaper").
- Indian domestic tariffs are slab-based with fixed charges (general; no specific board named).

## EXAMPLE FIGURES calculations
- Fan 75 W x 10 h = 0.75 kWh
- Bulbs 4 x 10 W x 5 h = 0.2 kWh
- TV 100 W x 4 h = 0.4 kWh
- Fridge = 1.2 kWh/day (assumed)
- Day = 2.55 units; month (30 d) = 76.5 units
- Energy charge: 76.5 x 7 = Rs 535.50
- Battery: 12 V x 150 Ah = 1800 Wh; 50% = 900 Wh; 900 x 0.85 / 150 W = 5.1 h (about five hours)
