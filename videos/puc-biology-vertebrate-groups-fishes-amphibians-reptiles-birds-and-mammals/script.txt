D1|0.35|First, split the vertebrates by one question. Does it have jaws?
D2|0.45|No jaws: the cyclostomes. Lampreys and hagfishes, with a round sucking mouth. They have no scales and no paired fins.
D3|0.45|With jaws: everyone else. They split again into fishes with fins, called Pisces, and the tetrapods. Tetra means four, and poda means feet.
D4|0.65|Fishes come in two classes. Tetrapods come in four: amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.
C1|0.35|Let's meet each class, one body at a time.
C2|0.5|Chondrichthyes, the cartilaginous fishes. The skeleton is soft cartilage, the mouth sits underneath, and the skin has tiny placoid scales. Example: Scoliodon, the dog fish.
C3|0.5|Osteichthyes, the bony fishes. A bony skeleton, a flap called the operculum over the gills, and an air bladder. Example: Labeo, the rohu.
C4|0.5|Amphibia live in water and on land. Moist skin with no scales, a tympanum for an ear, and a common opening called the cloaca. Example: Rana, the frog.
C5|0.5|Reptilia creep or crawl. Dry, horny skin with scales, and no outer ear opening. Example: Calotes, the garden lizard.
C6|0.5|Aves, the birds. Feathers, a beak, forelimbs turned into wings, and long bones that are hollow, with air inside. Example: Corvus, the crow.
C7|0.65|Mammalia. Hair on the skin, outer ears called pinnae, and mammary glands that make milk for the young. Example: Felis, the cat.
P1|0.35|Now, the heart. It changes as we climb the classes. Watch the blood.
P2|0.75|Step one. A fish heart has two chambers: one auricle that receives blood, and one ventricle that pumps it out.
P3|0.75|Step two. Amphibians and most reptiles have three chambers: two auricles and one ventricle.
P4|0.75|Step three. Crocodiles, birds and mammals have four chambers: two auricles and two ventricles.
P5|0.65|Birds and mammals are also warm blooded. Their body stays at the same temperature. Fishes, amphibians and reptiles are cold blooded. Their body warms and cools with the surroundings.
K1|0.35|The two fish classes are easy to mix up. Put a shark next to a rohu.
K2|0.45|The shark's skeleton is cartilage. The rohu's is bone.
K3|0.45|The shark's gill slits are separate, with no cover. The rohu's gills hide under the operculum.
K4|0.65|And the shark has no air bladder. The rohu has one, and it controls how high or low the fish floats.
K5|3.2|Your turn. Which one must keep swimming all the time, or it sinks? The shark, or the rohu? Pause and decide.
K6|0.65|The shark! With no air bladder to keep it afloat, a cartilaginous fish has to swim constantly to avoid sinking.
T1|0.6|Remember this. Two chambers, three chambers, four chambers. Count the heart, and you can place the class.
W1|0.35|Now, a worked example. Let's place Diya's four hostel animals.

W3|0.45|The lizard has dry skin covered with scales. Reptilia.
W4|0.45|The crow has feathers and hollow, air filled bones. Aves.
W5|0.65|The cat has hair, outer ears, and feeds its kittens milk. Mammalia.
