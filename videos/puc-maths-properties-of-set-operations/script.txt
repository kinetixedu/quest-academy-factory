H5|0.65|Sets follow a short list of laws. Learn them once, and long set expressions become short.
C1|0.4|First, order. A union B collects everything in A or in B. B union A collects exactly the same elements.
C2|0.4|So union is commutative. The order of the two sets does not matter. Intersection is commutative too.
C3|0.4|Now grouping. Take three sets. Join A and B first, then add C. Or join B and C first, then add A.
C4|0.65|Either way, you collect every element of all three. So union is associative. The brackets can move. Intersection is associative too.
C5|0.65|But recall episode eight. Difference is not commutative. A minus B and B minus A are different sets.
I1|0.4|Next, the special sets. The empty set adds nothing, so A union the empty set is A.
I2|0.4|And A union U is U, because U already holds everything.
I3|0.4|For intersection, it flips. A intersection the empty set is the empty set. A intersection U is A.
I4|0.65|And a set with itself. A union A is A, and A intersection A is A. Doing it twice changes nothing. These are called the idempotent laws.
D1|0.4|Now the big one, where union and intersection mix. The distributive law.
D2|0.4|It says: A intersection, B union C, equals A intersection B, union A intersection C.
D3|0.75|Let's test it with shading. Left side first. Shade B union C. Then keep only the part inside A.
D4|0.75|Now the right side. Shade A intersection B. Then add A intersection C.
D5|0.65|Both sides shade exactly the same three pieces. The picture works for any A, B and C, so the law always holds.
D6|0.6|It works the other way round too. A union, B intersection C, equals A union B, intersection A union C.
K1|0.4|Side by side. In numbers, multiplication spreads over addition. Two times, three plus four, is two times three, plus two times four.
K2|0.6|But addition does not spread over multiplication. Two plus, three times four, is fourteen. Two plus three, times two plus four, is thirty.
K3|3.2|Now you try. For sets, does union spread over intersection, the same way intersection spreads over union? Pause and decide.
K4|0.65|Yes! With sets, both distributive laws are true. That is one way sets are friendlier than numbers.
T1|0.7|Remember this. For union and intersection, order and brackets never matter, and each one spreads over the other.
M1|0.4|Last, the laws for complements, named after the mathematician Augustus De Morgan.
M2|0.75|Step one. The complement of A union B is everything outside both circles.
M3|0.75|Step two. Outside A is A complement. Outside B is B complement. Outside both is where these two overlap.
M4|0.75|Step three. So the complement of A union B equals A complement intersection B complement.
M5|0.65|In the same way, the complement of A intersection B equals A complement union B complement. The complement flips union into intersection.
M6|3.2|Quick one. Is the complement of A union B the same as A complement union B complement? Pause and think.
M7|0.65|No! A complement union B complement is the complement of A intersection B. It also covers the parts inside only one circle.
