H2|0.45|In a crowd, everyone does their own thing. In a team, players share the work. In a machine, every part has one fixed job.
H3|0.35|Aarav noticed this in the college corridor, and asked: do an animal's cells work like a crowd, a team, or a machine?
H4|0.5|It depends on the animal. Every animal has many cells, but they are not organised the same way.
H5|0.65|By the end, you will know the four levels of organisation, and two body systems exams love to compare.
B1|0.35|NCERT describes four levels.
B2|0.45|The cellular level. Cells work loosely, side by side, like a crowd.
B3|0.45|The tissue level. Cells doing the same job join into tissues, like a team.
B4|0.45|The organ level. Tissues group into organs, each for one particular job.
B5|0.65|The organ-system level. Organs link up into systems, like the parts of a machine.
Z1|0.35|Let's zoom into a sponge, the simplest case.
Z2|0.5|From outside, a sponge is a hollow body. Water enters through tiny pores, the ostia, and leaves through the osculum.
Z3|0.5|Zoom into the wall. The cells sit loosely, side by side. There are no true tissues and no organs.
Z4|0.5|Zoom again. This is a collar cell, or choanocyte. Its whip, the flagellum, beats and keeps the water moving.
Z5|0.65|Different cells do different jobs. This sharing of work is called division of labour. But the cells never form tissues. That is the cellular level.
L1|0.35|Now climb the four levels, with the NCERT examples.
L2|0.75|Step one. Cellular level. Loose groups of cells, with some division of labour. Example: sponges.
L3|0.75|Step two. Tissue level. Cells doing the same job are arranged into tissues. Example: coelenterates, like Hydra.
L4|0.75|Step three. Organ level. Tissues group together into organs, each one for a particular job. Example: the flatworms, Platyhelminthes.
L5|0.75|Step four. Organ-system level. Organs work together as systems, each with one body function. Examples: annelids, arthropods, molluscs, echinoderms and chordates.
L6|0.65|Each level is built from the one before.
T1|0.6|Remember this. Cells make tissues, tissues make organs, and organs make organ systems.
C1|0.35|Organ systems come in different designs. Start with the digestive system.
C2|0.45|A flatworm's gut has just one opening. Food goes in, and waste comes out, through the same hole. This is an incomplete digestive system.
C3|0.45|A roundworm or an earthworm has a tube with two openings: a mouth at the front and an anus at the back. This is a complete digestive system.
C4|0.65|In a tube, food moves one way, so each part does one job after another, like a factory line.
V1|0.35|Now the circulatory system. Blood has to reach every cell.
V2|0.45|In the open type, the heart pumps blood out, and the cells and tissues are bathed in it directly. Example: the arthropods.
V3|0.45|In the closed type, the blood always stays inside vessels: arteries, veins and capillaries. Examples: the annelids and the chordates, like us.
V4|0.65|Open is like water poured across a field. Closed is like water sent through pipes.
U1|3.2|Your turn. A honey bee is an arthropod. Is its circulation open or closed? Pause and decide.
U2|0.65|Open! Arthropods have an open circulatory system. The blood leaves the heart and bathes the tissues directly.
W1|0.35|Now, a worked example: four animals on the levels.
W2|0.45|Spongilla, a freshwater sponge. Loose cells. Cellular level.
W3|0.45|Hydra. Its cells form tissues. Tissue level.
W4|0.45|Fasciola, the liver fluke, a flatworm. It has organs, and a gut with one opening. Organ level, incomplete digestive system.
W5|0.65|Pheretima, the earthworm. Full organ systems, a gut with two openings, and blood inside vessels. Organ-system level, complete digestion, closed circulation.

X2|0.4|Many students think every animal at the organ-system level has closed circulation.
X3|0.65|But arthropods are at the organ-system level, and their circulation is open. The level tells how the organs are grouped, not the type of circulation.
