P2|0.4|Shine a torch straight down on a slanted stick. Its shadow on the floor is shorter than the stick.
P3|0.4|That shadow is the part of the stick that points along the floor.
P4|0.4|Now take two arrows, A and B. Find the shadow of A on B, and multiply it by the length of B.
P5|0.65|That is the dot product. The answer is just a number, so it is also called the scalar product.

S8|0.65|On the graph, as theta grows, the forward part falls. It follows cos theta.
D1|0.35|Now the formula, one small step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. Theta is the angle between A and B, measured tail to tail.
D3|0.75|Step two. The shadow of A on B is A cos theta. Cos theta is the fraction the shadow keeps. One at zero degrees, a half at sixty, zero at ninety.
D4|0.75|Step three. Multiply by the length of B. A dot B equals A B cos theta.
D5|0.75|Step four. The shadow of B on A, times A, gives the same number. So A dot B equals B dot A.
T1|0.7|Remember this. A dot B is one arrow times the shadow of the other. A number, not an arrow.
C1|0.35|Now, side by side. The angle decides the sign.
C2|0.4|At sixty degrees, the shadow of A falls forward, along B. The dot product is positive.
C3|0.6|At one hundred and twenty degrees, the shadow falls backward. The dot product is negative.
C4|3.2|Now you try. Theta is exactly ninety degrees. Is the dot product positive, negative, or zero? Pause and decide.
C5|0.65|Zero! At ninety degrees, the shadow shrinks to a single point. And cos ninety is zero.
R1|0.35|Now, a shortcut exams love. Follow the links.
R2|0.4|One. i hat and i hat point the same way. Theta is zero, so i dot i is one.
R3|0.4|Two. i hat and j hat are at ninety degrees, so i dot j is zero. Same for j and k, and k and i.
