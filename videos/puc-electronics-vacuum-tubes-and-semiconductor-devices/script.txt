S1|0.35|The answer was a semiconductor, like silicon.
S2|0.35|A semiconductor conducts better than an insulator, but not as well as a metal.
S3|0.65|Each silicon atom has four outer electrons, and shares them with four neighbours.
S4|0.65|Add a few phosphorus atoms. Phosphorus has five outer electrons, so one is left over and free to move. This is n-type silicon, with negative carriers.
S5|0.65|Add boron instead. Boron has only three, so one bond is missing an electron. That gap is a hole, and it acts like a positive charge. This is p-type silicon.
S6|0.65|Adding tiny impurities on purpose is called doping.
J1|0.35|Now join a p-type piece to an n-type piece. This is a p-n junction.

J3|0.65|It creates a small barrier, about zero point seven volts for silicon.
