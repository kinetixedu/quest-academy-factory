I2|0.65|Electronics works the same way in every field. A chain of blocks. Sense something, process the signal, then act on it.
O1|0.35|Let's follow one real chain in each field. First, communication. A phone call.
O2|0.35|The microphone turns your voice into a tiny electrical signal.
O3|0.35|An amplifier makes it stronger. The transmitter puts it onto a radio wave, and the antenna sends it out.
O4|0.65|At the other end, the chain runs backwards. Receiver, amplifier, speaker. Your friend hears your voice.
E1|0.35|Entertainment uses the same blocks. A television receives a signal, decodes the picture and the sound, and amplifies them for the screen and the speaker.
R1|0.35|Now, defence. Back to the fog. This is radar.
R2|0.35|The radar sends out a short, strong pulse of radio waves.
R3|0.35|The pulse hits the plane and bounces back. That faint echo is caught and amplified.
R4|0.65|Watch the scope. The big pulse goes out. A small echo comes back later. The gap between them is the key.
R5|0.35|Radio waves travel at the speed of light, c, three times ten to the eight metres per second.
R6|0.7|In time t, the pulse goes to the plane and comes back. So it covers twice the distance. Two d equals c times t.
R7|0.7|So d equals c times t, divided by two.
R8|0.65|Distance in metres, speed in metres per second, time in seconds.
W1|0.35|Worked example. The echo returns four hundred microseconds after the pulse.
W2|0.7|A microsecond is a millionth of a second. So t is four times ten to the minus four seconds.
W3|0.7|d equals three times ten to the eight, times four times ten to the minus four, divided by two.
W4|0.65|That is sixty thousand metres. The plane is sixty kilometres away.
N1|0.35|Next, industry. Think of an oven in a factory that must stay at one temperature.
N2|0.35|A sensor measures the temperature. A microcontroller compares it with the set value.
N3|0.65|Too cold, it switches the heater on. Too hot, it switches it off. This loop runs again and again, with no human needed. Robots work the same way.
D1|0.35|Finally, medicine. Diya's favourite.

D3|0.35|That signal is only about one millivolt. Far too small to see.

D5|0.65|The same field gives us scanners, monitors and pacemakers that keep hearts beating on time.
