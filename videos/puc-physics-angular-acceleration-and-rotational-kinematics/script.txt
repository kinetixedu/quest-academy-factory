P1|0.35|Angular acceleration, alpha, tells how fast omega changes: the change in omega, divided by the time.
P2|0.4|Its unit is radians per second squared.
P3|0.4|For a fixed axis, alpha lies along the axis too. Speeding up, it points the same way as omega. Slowing down, the opposite way.
P4|0.6|And every point of a rigid body has the same alpha, just like the same omega.

D1|0.35|Now the equations. They are old friends in new clothes.
D2|0.75|Step one. Alpha is d omega by d t, just as a is d v by d t.
D3|0.75|Step two. With a steady alpha, omega equals omega nought plus alpha t. Like v equals u plus a t.
D4|0.75|Step three. The angle is the area under the omega line. Theta equals omega nought t, plus half alpha t squared.
D5|0.75|Step four. Remove t, and you get omega squared equals omega nought squared, plus two alpha theta.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Swap x for theta, v for omega, and a for alpha. The same equations work for turning.
