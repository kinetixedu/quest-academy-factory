H5|0.6|The answer is torque. Soon you will know why handles sit far from the hinge.
P1|0.35|A force makes a thing move. Torque makes a thing turn.
P2|0.35|Torque is the turning effect of a force about an axis, the line a body turns around.
P3|0.4|For a door, the axis is the hinge. For a nut, the bolt. For a pedal, the axle.
P4|0.6|Torque depends on three things. How hard you push. How far from the axis. And at what angle.
S1|0.35|Let's test it on our motion notebook. Aarav's door, seen from above. The hinge is on the left.
S2|0.4|Every push is the same: twenty newtons, at right angles.
S3|0.4|Push at zero point three metres from the hinge. The torque is six newton metres. The door turns a little.
S4|0.4|Push at zero point six metres. Twice as far, twice the torque. Twelve newton metres.
S5|0.5|Push at the handle, zero point nine metres. Eighteen newton metres. The door swings the most.
S6|0.6|On the graph, torque grows in a straight line with distance.
S7|0.35|Slow-mo replay. Freeze at the push.
S8|0.4|The arrow from the hinge to the hand is r, the position vector. The push F is at right angles to it.
S9|0.4|Now push at the handle at a slant, partly toward the hinge. The angle between r and F is one hundred and fifty degrees.
S10|0.6|The torque drops to nine newton metres. Only part of a slanted push turns the door.
D1|0.35|Let's build the formula, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. Torque is a vector. It is r cross F.
D3|0.75|Step two. Its size is r F sine theta, where theta is the angle between r and F.
D4|0.75|Step three. r sine theta is the moment arm: the shortest distance from the axis to the line of the force. So torque is the moment arm times F.
D5|0.75|Step four. The unit is the newton metre. The dimensions are M L squared T to the minus two.
D6|0.6|The direction comes from the right hand rule. Curl your fingers from r to F. Your thumb gives the torque, out of the screen.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Torque is the force times the perpendicular distance from the axis to its line.
R1|0.35|So why is the handle far from the hinge? Link by link.
R2|0.4|Your push is about the same every time. But the handle is far, so the moment arm is big.
R3|0.6|A big arm gives a big torque. So the door turns easily.
R4|0.6|Flip it around. For the same nine newton metres, at the handle you need ten newtons. Halfway, twenty. Near the hinge, sixty.
R5|3.1|Now you try. Which turns a stuck bolt more: forty newtons at zero point two metres, or twenty newtons at zero point five? Pause and decide.
R6|0.65|Twenty at zero point five! That gives ten newton metres. Forty at zero point two gives only eight.
K1|0.4|Side by side. Push at right angles, and the moment arm is the full distance r.

X1|0.35|Now, the exam trap.
X2|0.4|Many students write torque as F times r, the distance to the point where the force acts.
