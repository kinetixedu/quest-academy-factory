D1|0.35|Where does m r squared come from? From kinetic energy, step by step.
D2|0.75|Step one. A bit of mass m, at a distance r from the axis, moves at speed v equals r omega.
D3|0.75|Step two. Its kinetic energy is half m v squared. That is half m r squared omega squared.
D4|0.75|Step three. Add up every bit. All the bits share the same omega, so take it outside the sum.
D5|0.75|Step four. K equals half, times the sum of m r squared, times omega squared. That sum is I.
D6|0.6|So K equals half I omega squared. Just like half m v squared, with I in place of mass.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Moment of inertia is the turning version of mass. I equals the sum of m r squared.
R1|0.35|Now, why the end is harder, link by link.
R2|0.4|About the end, the far ball swings round a big circle.
R3|0.4|A bigger circle in the same time means a bigger speed. v equals r omega.
R4|0.4|Kinetic energy grows with speed squared. So it grows with r squared.
R5|0.6|So the same spin needs more energy. That is a bigger moment of inertia.
