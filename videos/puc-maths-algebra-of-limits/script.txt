H5|0.65|Yes, we can. The rules that let us do it are called the algebra of limits.
B1|0.35|First, two tiny limits. They are the building blocks.
B2|0.4|The limit of x, as x tends to a, is just a. If x creeps towards two, then x itself gets close to two.
B3|0.65|The limit of a constant is that constant. If a function always gives five, it stays five, near every point.
L1|0.35|Now watch two pieces at work.
L2|0.4|Let f of x be x squared plus three x. And let g of x be x plus two.
L3|0.4|As x creeps towards two, f closes in on ten. And g closes in on four.
L4|0.4|Add them. f plus g closes in on fourteen. Exactly ten plus four.
L5|0.4|Multiply them. f times g closes in on forty. Exactly ten times four.
L6|0.65|Divide them. f over g closes in on two point five. Exactly ten over four.
R1|0.4|This always works. NCERT gives it as a theorem. Suppose the limits of f and g at a both exist.
R2|0.75|Rule one. The limit of a sum is the sum of the limits.
R3|0.75|Rule two. The limit of a difference is the difference of the limits.
R4|0.75|Rule three. The limit of a product is the product of the limits.
R5|0.75|Rule four. The limit of a quotient is the quotient of the limits. But only when the limit of the bottom is not zero.
R6|0.65|And a number in front just comes along. The limit of k times f is k times the limit of f.

S1|0.4|Side by side. In our example the bottom, x plus two, tends to four. Four is not zero, so the quotient rule works.
S2|0.6|But look at x squared minus four, over x minus two, as x tends to two. The bottom tends to zero. Here the quotient rule is switched off.

D1|0.35|Now let us crack the scary limit, one small step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. x squared is x times x. So it tends to two times two, which is four.
D3|0.75|Step two. So x squared plus three x tends to four plus six, which is ten.
D4|0.75|Step three. x minus one tends to one. By the product rule, the top tends to ten times one, which is ten.
D5|0.75|Step four. The bottom, x plus two, tends to four. Four is not zero, so we may divide.
D6|0.65|Step five. The limit is ten over four, which is two point five. No table needed.
