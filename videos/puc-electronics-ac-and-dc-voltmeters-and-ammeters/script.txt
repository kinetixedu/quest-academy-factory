G1|0.35|Inside every analogue meter sits a moving coil galvanometer.
G2|0.35|A small coil of wire sits between the poles of a strong magnet.
G3|0.35|Send current through the coil, and the magnet pushes its two sides in opposite directions. The coil turns.
G4|0.35|A tiny spring pulls back. The needle stops where the two balance.
G5|0.65|Double the current, and the needle turns twice as far. The deflection is proportional to the current.
G6|0.65|Two numbers describe every galvanometer. The coil resistance, G. And the full scale current, I g, the current that swings the needle all the way.
B1|0.35|Now let's turn it into an ammeter on the breadboard.
B2|0.35|A big current would burn the thin coil. So we connect a tiny resistance in parallel with it. This is called a shunt.
B3|0.35|Watch the electrons. At the junction, almost all of them take the easy path through the shunt. Only a few pass through the coil.
B4|0.35|Turn up the current. The coil still gets only its small share, and the needle stays on the scale.
B5|0.65|An ammeter is connected in series, and its resistance must be very low, so it does not change the current it measures.
D1|0.35|So how big should the shunt be? Let's work it out.
D2|0.7|The coil and the shunt are in parallel, so they have the same voltage across them.
D3|0.7|The voltage across the coil is I g times G.
D4|0.7|The rest of the current, I minus I g, flows through the shunt S. So its voltage is I minus I g, times S.
D5|0.7|Set the two equal, and S equals I g times G, divided by I minus I g.
D6|0.65|Resistances in ohms, currents in amperes. Bigger range, smaller shunt.
V1|0.35|Now a voltmeter. Here we need the opposite.
V2|0.35|We add a large resistance in series with the coil. This is called a multiplier.
V3|0.65|A voltmeter is connected in parallel, across the part we test. Its resistance must be very high, so it draws almost no current.
V4|0.7|At full scale, the current I g flows through G and R together. So V equals I g times G plus R.
V5|0.65|Rearrange it, and R equals V divided by I g, minus G.
