M3|0.45|Fungi leave Plantae too. They cannot make food, and their walls are made of chitin. Kingdom Fungi.
M4|0.45|Chlamydomonas and Chlorella were called algae, inside Plantae. Both have cell walls.
M5|0.45|Amoeba and Paramoecium were called animals. Both lack cell walls.

M7|0.65|What stays behind in Plantae and Animalia are the many celled plants and animals.
L1|0.35|Now let's build Whittaker's comparison table, one row at a time.
L2|0.75|Row one, cell type. Monera are prokaryotic. The other four kingdoms are eukaryotic.
L3|0.75|Row two, cell wall. Monera have a wall, but not of cellulose. Some protists have walls. Fungi have chitin. Plants have cellulose. Animals have no cell wall.
L4|0.75|Row three, nuclear membrane. It is absent only in Monera, and present in the other four.
L5|0.75|Row four, body organisation. Monera and Protista are just cells. Fungi are many celled, with loose tissue. Plants have tissues and organs. Animals go further, to organ systems.
L6|0.75|Row five, mode of nutrition. Monera can make food or take it. So can Protista. Fungi are heterotrophic. Plants are autotrophic. Animals are heterotrophic.
