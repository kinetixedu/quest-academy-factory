P3|0.4|Turn an angle theta from the positive x axis. The arm ends at a point P on the circle.
P4|0.4|The x coordinate of P is cosine theta. The y coordinate is sine theta.
P5|0.65|So cosine tells you left or right. Sine tells you up or down. Right and up are plus. Left and down are minus.
S1|0.4|Now let P travel all the way round, and watch the signs.
S2|0.5|First quadrant, theta from zero to pi by two. P is to the right and above. Cosine is plus, sine is plus.
S3|0.5|Second quadrant, from pi by two to pi. P crosses to the left. Cosine turns minus, but sine stays plus.
S4|0.5|Third quadrant, from pi to three pi by two. P is left and below. Cosine and sine are both minus.
S5|0.5|Fourth quadrant, from three pi by two to two pi. P comes back to the right, but stays below. Cosine is plus again, sine is minus.
S6|0.65|Look at the sine graph drawn beside the circle. It stays above the axis for the first half turn, and below it for the second half.
D1|0.35|What about the other four functions? They are all built from sine and cosine, so their signs follow.
D2|0.75|Step one. Tangent is sine divided by cosine.
D3|0.75|Step two. When you divide, same signs give plus, and opposite signs give minus.
D4|0.75|Step three. Sine and cosine have the same sign only in the first and third quadrants. So tangent is plus there, and minus in the other two.
D5|0.75|Step four. Cosecant is one over sine, so it copies the sign of sine. Secant copies cosine. Cotangent copies tangent.
D6|0.65|And the whole sign table is complete.
