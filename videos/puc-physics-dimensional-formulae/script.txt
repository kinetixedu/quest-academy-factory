P1|0.35|So what is a dimensional formula?
P2|0.4|It shows how a quantity depends on mass, length and time, and with which powers.
P3|0.4|We write it inside square brackets, as M to the a, L to the b, T to the c.
P4|0.4|Written as an equation, like velocity equals L T to the minus one, it is a dimensional equation.
P5|0.65|The trick is simple. Start from a formula you know, and replace every quantity by its dimensions.
S1|0.35|Let's watch the cooker lid on our motion notebook.
S2|0.4|Steam pushes up on the lid. That push is a force, F. It is spread over the area of the lid, A.
S3|0.4|As the steam heats up, the force grows. Pressure is force divided by area, so the pressure grows too.
S4|0.45|At a certain pressure, the weight lifts. Let's replay that in slow motion. Force up beats the weight down.
S5|0.5|The weight jumps, steam escapes, and the pressure drops. Then it builds again.
S6|0.65|On the graph, pressure climbs, drops, and climbs again. Every whistle is one peak.
D1|0.35|Now let's build the dimensional formula of pressure, block by block.
D2|0.75|Step one. Velocity is displacement over time. L T to the minus one.
D3|0.75|Step two. Acceleration is velocity over time. L T to the minus two.
D4|0.75|Step three. Force is mass times acceleration. M L T to the minus two.
D5|0.75|Step four. Pressure is force over area. Area is L squared.
D6|0.65|M L T to the minus two, divided by L squared, is M L to the minus one T to the minus two.
R1|0.35|Let's check each power, link by link.
R2|0.4|Mass came only from the force. One M on top. So the power of M is one.
R3|0.4|Length: one L on top from the force, two below from the area. One minus two is minus one.
R4|0.6|Time came only from the acceleration, divided by time twice. So the power of T is minus two.
T1|0.75|Remember this. To find a dimensional formula, start from a known formula and replace each quantity by its dimensions.
K1|0.4|Side by side. Force is M L T to the minus two. It is the total push.
K2|0.6|Pressure is M L to the minus one T to the minus two. The push on each square metre. Only the power of L changes.
K3|3.2|Now you try. A small pin and a big plate get the same push. Which one gets the bigger pressure? Pause and decide.
K4|0.65|The pin! Area sits below the line, so a tiny area means a big pressure for the same force.
G1|0.35|With the same blocks, we can build energy and work.
G2|0.4|Work is force times distance. M L T to the minus two, times L, gives M L squared T to the minus two.
G3|0.4|Kinetic energy is half m v squared. The half is a pure number, so it has no dimension.
G4|3.2|Your turn. Does half m v squared have the same dimensional formula as work? Pause and decide.
G5|0.65|Yes! M times L T to the minus one, squared, is M L squared T to the minus two. That is why both use the joule.
W1|0.35|Now, one worked example.

W4|0.75|Step two. Time is T.

W6|0.65|So power is M L squared T to the minus three. Its unit, the watt, is one joule per second.
