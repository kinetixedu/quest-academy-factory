P1|0.35|First, recall resonance. When one Lewis drawing is not enough, the real molecule is a hybrid of several drawings.
P2|0.35|For this, a molecule needs a conjugated system. That means single and double bonds taking turns, like a zebra crossing.
P3|0.35|A lone pair next to a double bond also counts. Then electrons can flow along the whole system.
P4|0.35|When a group pushes or pulls electrons along this system, we call it the resonance effect, or mesomeric effect.
P5|0.65|Groups that push electrons in show plus R. Groups that pull electrons out show minus R.
S1|0.35|Let's watch it on our molecule bench. Here is aniline. A benzene ring with an N H two group.
S2|0.4|The nitrogen has a lone pair, right next to the ring's double bonds. Watch the curved arrows.
S3|0.4|The lone pair moves in to make a new bond, and a ring pi pair moves onto a carbon. That carbon, next to the group, is the ortho carbon. It gets a minus charge.
S4|0.4|Another shift, and the minus charge moves to the opposite carbon, the para position. Then to the other ortho carbon.
S5|0.4|So in aniline, the ortho and para carbons become richer in electrons. N H two is a plus R group.
S6|0.4|Plus R groups include halogens, O H, O R, N H two, and the other amino groups.
S7|0.65|Now nitrobenzene. The nitro group pulls the ring's electrons out, so ortho and para become poorer, with plus charges. Minus R groups include C O O H, C H O, the C O group, C N, and N O two.
D1|0.35|Now Aarav's question. How do C H bonds help a carbocation? Step by step.
D2|0.75|Step one. In the ethyl cation, the plus carbon has an empty p orbital, an empty seat for electrons.
D3|0.75|Step two. The carbon next to it holds C H bonds. These are called alpha hydrogens.
D4|0.75|Step three. The electron pair of one C H bond leans over and partly fills the empty seat. The plus charge gets shared.
D5|0.75|Step four. Sharing the charge makes the ion more stable. This spreading of sigma electrons is called hyperconjugation. It is also called no bond resonance.
D6|0.65|Hyperconjugation also works next to a double bond, as in propene.
K1|0.35|Now count the helpers. Each alpha hydrogen is one helper.
K2|0.4|The methyl cation has zero alpha hydrogens. The ethyl cation has three. The isopropyl cation has six. The tertiary butyl cation has nine.
K3|0.65|More helpers, more spreading, more stability. That is why the tertiary carbocation is the most stable.
