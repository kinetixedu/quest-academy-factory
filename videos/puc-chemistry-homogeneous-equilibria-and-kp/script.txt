P1|0.35|First, a new word: homogeneous. It means the same all through.
P2|0.4|Think of a smoothie. Everything is blended into one. You cannot see separate pieces.
P3|0.6|In a homogeneous equilibrium, every reactant and product is in the same phase. All gases, or all dissolved in one solution.
S1|0.35|Let's put nitrogen and hydrogen on our molecule bench, in a closed tank, to make ammonia.
S2|0.4|All three are gases. They mix completely. One phase. A homogeneous equilibrium.
S3|0.4|Every molecule hits the walls. Those hits make pressure.
S4|0.4|Each gas pushes with its own share. That share is its partial pressure.
S5|0.4|Add up all the partial pressures, and you get the total pressure in the tank.
S6|0.6|The same idea works in a solution, like iron ions and thiocyanate ions, all dissolved in water.
L1|0.35|Now build K p. Same recipe as K c, but with partial pressures.
L2|0.4|Products on top, reactants below, each raised to its power.
L3|0.65|For ammonia, K p is the pressure of ammonia squared, over nitrogen times hydrogen cubed. Pressures in bar.
D1|0.35|How are K p and K c linked? Step by step.
D2|0.75|Step one. The ideal gas equation. p V equals n R T.
D3|0.75|Step two. Divide by V. Pressure equals n over V, times R T. And n over V is the concentration.
D4|0.75|Step three. So every pressure is its concentration times R T. Put that into K p.
D5|0.75|Step four. The R T terms collect into one power. K p equals K c times R T, to the power delta n.
G1|0.35|What is delta n?
G2|0.4|Delta n is moles of gas on the product side, minus moles of gas on the reactant side.
G3|0.4|For ammonia: two minus four. Delta n is minus two.
G4|0.4|For hydrogen iodide: two minus two. Zero. So K p equals K c.
G5|0.6|Use R equal to zero point zero eight three one bar litre per mole per kelvin, with T in kelvin.
C1|0.4|Compare. Hydrogen iodide: delta n zero, K p equals K c. Ammonia: delta n minus two, K p is K c divided by R T squared.
K1|3.2|Pause and think. For two S O two plus O two making two S O three, at five hundred kelvin, is K p bigger or smaller than K c?
K2|0.65|Smaller. Delta n is minus one, so K p is K c divided by R T, and R T is about forty two.
R1|0.35|Now, the units of K.
R2|0.4|K c has units of moles per litre, to the power delta n. K p has units of bar, to the power delta n.
R3|0.4|When delta n is zero, K has no units at all.
R4|0.6|In thermodynamics, each value is divided by its standard value, one mole per litre or one bar. Then K has no units.
