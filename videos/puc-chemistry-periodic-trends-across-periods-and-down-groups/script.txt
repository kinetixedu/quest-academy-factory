P1|0.35|So what is a periodic trend?
P2|0.35|A trend is a property that changes steadily as you move across a period, or down a group.
P3|0.35|Across a period, the nucleus gains protons, but new electrons join the same shell. The pull grows, and atoms shrink.
P4|0.6|Down a group, a new shell is added each time. The outer electrons sit farther out, and atoms grow.
P5|0.65|Size and pull. Almost every trend comes from these two.
S1|0.35|Let's light up the table, one trend at a time. First, atomic radius.
S2|0.4|It shrinks across a period, from lithium at one hundred and fifty two picometres to fluorine at sixty four. It grows down a group, up to caesium at two hundred and sixty two.
S3|0.4|Second, ionization enthalpy, the energy to remove an electron. It rises across a period, from lithium, five hundred and twenty, to neon, two thousand and eighty one kilojoules per mole. It falls down a group.
S4|0.4|Third, electron gain enthalpy, the energy change when an atom takes in an electron. It becomes more negative across a period, and less negative down a group.
S5|0.4|Fourth, electronegativity, the pull on a shared pair. It rises across a period, and falls down a group. Fluorine is the top, at four.
S6|0.65|Fifth, metallic character. Metals lose electrons easily. So metallic character falls across a period, and rises down a group.
B1|0.4|Notice the pattern. Radius and metallic character grow towards the bottom left. The other three, towards the top right.
B2|3.2|Pause and think. Which is more metallic, potassium or magnesium?
B3|0.65|Potassium! It sits further left and further down. Both moves make an element more metallic.
D1|0.35|Now, why are the two ends of a period the most reactive? Step by step.
D2|0.75|Step one. On the far left, ionization enthalpy is the lowest. Alkali metals lose one electron easily, and form positive ions, called cations.
D3|0.75|Step two. On the far right, electron gain enthalpy is the most negative. Halogens take an electron easily, and form negative ions, called anions.
D4|0.75|Step three. In the middle, atoms neither lose nor gain electrons easily. So reactivity is lowest in the centre.
D5|0.75|Step four. Noble gases have full shells, and a positive electron gain enthalpy. They hardly react at all.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Small atoms pull hard. Big atoms let go easily. Every trend follows from that.
