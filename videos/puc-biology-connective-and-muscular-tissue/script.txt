L1|0.35|Now let's sort connective tissue, step by step.
L2|0.75|Step one. Loose connective tissue has cells and fibres loosely spread in a jelly. Areolar tissue, under the skin, is a support framework for the skin.
L3|0.75|Step two. Adipose tissue is also loose. It lies mainly under the skin, and its cells store fat.
L4|0.75|Step three. Dense connective tissue has fibres and fibroblasts packed tight. Tendons join muscle to bone. Ligaments join bone to bone.
L5|0.75|Step four. Cartilage is solid but bendy. Its cells, the chondrocytes, sit in small spaces. It is at the tip of the nose, in the outer ear, and between the bones of the backbone.
L6|0.75|Step five. Bone is hard and does not bend. Its matrix is rich in calcium salts and collagen. Its cells, the osteocytes, sit in spaces called lacunae.

D1|0.35|Now the second family: muscle tissue.
D2|0.45|A muscle is made of long, thin fibres, side by side. Each fibre is packed with even thinner threads, the myofibrils.
D3|0.45|When a signal arrives, the fibres contract. They get shorter and thicker, and pull.
D4|0.65|Then they relax and get longer again. Contract, relax, again and again. That is how the biceps bends your arm.

U3|0.6|Cardiac muscle is found only in the heart. Its cells are joined by intercalated discs, so they contract together, as one unit.
