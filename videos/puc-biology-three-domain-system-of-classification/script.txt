B1|0.35|First, where we start from.
B2|0.45|In the five kingdom system, all organisms without a nucleus sit together in one kingdom, Monera.
B3|0.45|A cell without a true nucleus is a prokaryote. Its DNA lies loose in the cell. A cell with a nucleus is a eukaryote.
B4|0.65|The other four kingdoms, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia, are all eukaryotes. So Monera looked like one simple group.
Z1|0.35|Then a scientist named Carl Woese looked deeper, at one part found in every cell.
Z2|0.5|Every living cell has ribosomes. These are tiny parts that build proteins.
Z3|0.5|Zoom in. A ribosome is made of protein and a special RNA, called ribosomal RNA.
Z4|0.5|Zoom again. Ribosomal RNA is a long chain of four kinds of building blocks, written as letters: A, U, G and C.
Z5|0.65|All cells need ribosomes, so all cells carry this molecule. That makes it a fair way to compare any two living things.
S1|0.35|Now watch how the comparison works.
S2|0.75|Step one. Read the order of letters in the ribosomal RNA of each organism.
S3|0.75|Step two. Line the chains up side by side, letter by letter.
S4|0.75|Step three. Count the places where the letters differ. Few differences mean close relatives. Many differences mean distant ones.
S5|0.75|Step four. Join the closest ones first, and a family tree of life grows.
S6|0.65|Woese found that the microbes from harsh places differed from ordinary bacteria as much as bacteria differ from us.
D1|0.35|So in nineteen ninety, Woese and his co-workers proposed three domains.
D2|0.45|A domain is a rank above kingdom. It is the biggest group of all.
D3|0.45|Domain Bacteria holds the true bacteria, like the ones that turn milk into curd.
D4|0.45|Domain Archaea holds the microbes of harsh places: salty water, hot springs, and marshes.
D5|0.65|Domain Eukarya holds every organism with a nucleus. Protists, fungi, plants and animals all sit here.
C1|0.35|Now compare the two domains without a nucleus.
C2|0.45|Bacteria. No nucleus. Most have a cell wall built with a material called peptidoglycan. Example: the curd bacteria.
C3|0.45|Archaea. Also no nucleus. But their cell wall is different, and this helps them survive harsh places. Example: methanogens, which make methane gas in the gut of cows and buffaloes.
C4|3.2|Your turn. A microbe lives in a salt lake, in water far saltier than the sea. Bacteria or Archaea? Pause and decide.
C5|0.65|Archaea! Microbes of extremely salty places are called halophiles, and they belong to Archaea.
T1|0.6|Remember this. Three domains: two without a nucleus, and one with.
W1|0.35|Now, a worked example. Let's sort six organisms into the three domains.
W2|0.45|The curd bacterium has no nucleus and a normal bacterial wall. Domain Bacteria.
W3|0.45|A methanogen in a cow's gut. No nucleus, a different wall. Domain Archaea. So is a halophile from a salt pan.
W4|0.45|Yeast has a nucleus. Domain Eukarya. So do the amoeba and the mango tree.
W5|0.65|Notice the old kingdom Monera has split in two. Bacteria and Archaea now stand in separate domains.
