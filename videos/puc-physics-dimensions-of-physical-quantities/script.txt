P2|0.4|Every physical quantity is built from the seven base quantities.
P3|0.4|In mechanics, three of them are enough. Mass, length and time.
P4|0.4|We write them with square brackets. Capital M for mass, capital L for length, capital T for time.
P5|0.65|The dimensions of a quantity are the powers of the base quantities needed to build it.
S1|0.35|Let's watch Aarav's bicycle on our motion notebook.
S2|0.4|It covers five metres in each second. Distance is a length. Its dimension is L.
S3|0.4|Time is T. Speed is distance divided by time. So speed is L over T, or L T to the minus one.
S4|0.45|Now he speeds up. Let's replay it in slow motion. Every second, the speed grows by two metres per second.
S5|0.5|Acceleration is a change in speed, divided by time. So it is L T to the minus one, divided by T again.

D1|0.35|Let's build the dimensions of area, step by step.
D2|0.75|Step one. Area equals length times breadth.
D3|0.75|Step two. Length is L. Breadth is also a length, so it is L too.
D4|0.75|Step three. L times L is L squared. So area has the dimension L squared.
D5|0.75|Step four. No mass, no time. So we write M to the zero, L squared, T to the zero.
D6|0.65|The zero powers say: no mass, no time. Only length, twice.
R1|0.35|Now the reasoning for volume and density, link by link.
R2|0.4|Volume is length times breadth times height. Three lengths. So L cubed.
R3|0.4|Density is mass divided by volume. Mass is M. Volume is L cubed.
R4|0.6|So density is M over L cubed. That is M L to the minus three. A negative power means we divided.
T1|0.75|Remember this. Dimensions are the powers of M, L and T. You can only add or subtract quantities with the same dimensions.
