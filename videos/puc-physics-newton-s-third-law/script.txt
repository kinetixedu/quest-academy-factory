H5|0.65|The answer is Newton's third law. And it hides a trap that catches almost everyone.
P1|0.35|Here is the law.
P2|0.4|To every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction.
P3|0.4|In plain words, forces always come in pairs. If A pushes B, then B pushes A back.
P4|0.4|The two forces have the same size and opposite directions, and they act at the same instant. Neither one comes first.
P5|0.65|So the table pushed Aarav back, just as hard as he pushed it. His chair rolls easily. The table's legs grip the floor.

D1|0.35|Now let's write it down, one step at a time.
D2|0.75|Step one. Write F sub A B for the force on A by B, and F sub B A for the force on B by A.
D3|0.75|Step two. The third law says F sub A B equals minus F sub B A. Same size, opposite direction.
D4|0.75|Step three. The two forces act on different bodies. So use the second law on each body on its own. The acceleration of A is F over m A. Of B, F over m B.
D5|0.75|Step four. Divide them. a A over a B equals m B over m A. The lighter body gets the bigger acceleration.
D6|0.6|For our carts, that is eight over two. Four times the acceleration for A.
T1|0.7|Remember this. Equal and opposite, but on two different bodies.
K1|0.4|Here is why that matters. Side by side.
K2|0.4|On the left, a book on a table. Its weight pulls it down. The table pushes it up. Both forces act on the book, so they cancel.
K3|0.6|On the right, an action reaction pair. The book pushes down on the table. The table pushes up on the book. One force on each body, so they never cancel.
K4|3.2|Now you try. The book's weight and the table's push are equal and opposite. Are they an action reaction pair? Pause and decide.
K5|0.65|No! Both act on the book. The partner of its weight is the book pulling the Earth up, with the same force.
