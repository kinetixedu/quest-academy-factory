P1|0.35|Now watch what a meristem does, one cell at a time.
P2|0.75|Step one. A meristem cell grows a little, and copies what is in its nucleus.
P3|0.75|Step two. It divides into two new cells.
P4|0.75|Step three. This happens again and again. New cells pile up behind the tip, and the tip moves on.
P5|0.75|Step four. The cells left behind grow bigger and change shape. Each becomes special for one job, like carrying water.
P6|0.75|Step five. Now they lose the power to divide. They are called permanent cells, and they form the permanent tissues.
C1|0.35|Plants have three kinds of meristem. Each sits in a different place.
C2|0.5|Apical meristems sit at the tips of roots and shoots. They make the plant longer.
C3|0.5|Intercalary meristems sit between mature tissues. They occur in grasses, and regrow the parts that grazing animals eat. That is how Diya's lawn grew back.
C4|0.5|Lateral meristems sit along the sides of older roots and stems, shaped like a hollow cylinder. They make woody plants thicker.
C5|0.65|And as the shoot grows, some cells are left behind by the shoot apical meristem. They form the axillary bud, in the angle between a leaf and the stem. It can grow into a branch, or a flower.
V1|0.75|Side by side. When they appear. Apical and intercalary meristems appear early in the life of a plant. They are primary meristems. Lateral meristems appear later, in older parts. They are secondary meristems.
V2|0.75|What they build. Primary meristems build the primary plant body. Secondary meristems build secondary tissues, and make the plant thicker.
