L1|0.35|So how does a trunk get thicker? Follow it step by step.
L2|0.75|Step one. Between the bundles, parenchyma cells are living, mature and not dividing.
L3|0.75|Step two. Some of them get back the power to divide. This is dedifferentiation. They form the interfascicular cambium.
L4|0.75|Step three. This joins the cambium inside the bundles, making a complete ring.
L5|0.75|Step four. The ring makes new cells. These lose the power to divide again, and mature to do a job, like carrying water. This is redifferentiation.
L6|0.75|Step five. Near the surface, cork cambium also forms from parenchyma by dedifferentiation. Year after year, the stem grows thicker.
C1|0.35|How does a new cell know what to become?
C2|0.45|In plants, differentiation is open. The same meristem can make different kinds of cells.
C3|0.65|Cells pushed out ahead of the root apical meristem become root cap cells. Cells pushed to the side become epidermis. The final structure depends on where the cell sits.
D1|0.35|Now put it all together. This is development.

D3|0.45|A cell divides, grows, differentiates and matures. Then it ages and dies. A mature cell can loop back by dedifferentiation, and divide again.
