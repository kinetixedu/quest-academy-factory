K1|0.35|So what makes a fungus?
K2|0.75|Fungi are heterotrophic. They cannot make their own food. Except for yeasts, which are single cells, they are made of hyphae.
K3|0.75|Most absorb food from dead matter. They are saprophytes. Some feed on living plants and animals. They are parasites.
K4|0.75|Some live in partnership. With algae, they form lichens. With the roots of plants, they form mycorrhiza.
K5|0.75|Fungi love warm, humid places. That is why we keep food in the fridge.
K6|0.65|Yeast makes bread and beer. Penicillium gives us antibiotics. But Puccinia causes wheat rust.
L1|0.35|Fungi are sorted into four classes, by the mycelium, how the spores form, and the fruiting body.
L2|0.75|Class one, Phycomycetes. They live in water, on damp decaying wood, or as parasites on plants. The mycelium has no cross walls. It is coenocytic.
L3|0.75|Their asexual spores form inside a sporangium. Two gametes fuse to form a zygospore. Examples: Mucor, Rhizopus, the bread mould, and Albugo, on mustard leaves.
L4|0.75|Class two, Ascomycetes, the sac fungi. The mycelium is branched, with cross walls. Asexual spores called conidia form outside, on conidiophores. Sexual ascospores form inside a sac called an ascus.
L5|0.75|Examples: Penicillium, yeast, Aspergillus, Claviceps and Neurospora. Morels and truffles are edible delicacies.
L6|0.75|Class three, Basidiomycetes. Mushrooms, bracket fungi, puffballs, rusts and smuts. Four basidiospores form outside, on a club shaped basidium. Examples: Agaricus, Ustilago and Puccinia.
L7|0.65|Class four, Deuteromycetes, the imperfect fungi. Only their asexual or vegetative stages are known. They reproduce by conidia. Examples: Alternaria, Colletotrichum and Trichoderma.
P1|0.35|Two classes are easy to mix up. Let's compare them.
P2|0.45|Ascomycetes, like Penicillium. The sexual spores form inside a sac, the ascus. Inside is called endogenous.
P3|0.6|Basidiomycetes, like Agaricus, the mushroom. The spores form outside, on a basidium. Outside is called exogenous.
