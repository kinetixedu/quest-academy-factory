L1|0.35|Now, how fast does growth happen? There are two patterns.
L2|0.45|In arithmetic growth, after a cell divides, only one daughter cell keeps dividing. The other one grows up and matures.
L3|0.45|So the root gets longer by the same amount each time. Plot length against time and you get a straight line. Length at time t equals starting length plus r times t.
L4|0.45|In geometric growth, both daughter cells keep dividing. One cell becomes two, then four, then eight.
L5|0.45|At first, growth is slow. This is the lag phase. Then it shoots up fast. This is the log, or exponential, phase.
L6|0.45|But nutrients run short, so growth slows down and stops. This is the stationary phase. The whole curve looks like the letter S, so it is called a sigmoid curve.
L7|0.65|Here, final size equals starting size times e to the power r t. The r is the relative growth rate. It shows how well a plant makes new material, so it is also called the efficiency index.
