H3|0.35|Diya asked: how do botanists describe a flower so exactly that anyone can picture it?
H4|0.5|They ask three questions. What rings of parts does it have? How is it shaped? And where does the ovary sit?
H5|0.65|By the end, you will read any flower with these three questions.
D1|0.45|First, the parts. A flower sits on a stalk called the pedicel. Its swollen top is the thalamus, also called the receptacle.
D2|0.6|On the thalamus grow four whorls. A whorl is a ring of parts, one ring inside the next.
D3|0.6|The calyx is made of sepals, the outermost ring, usually green. The corolla is made of petals, often bright, to attract insects.
D4|0.6|The androecium is made of stamens, the male parts. The gynoecium is made of carpels, the female parts, with the ovary at the bottom.
D5|0.65|In some flowers, like lily, the sepals and petals look alike. Together they are called the perianth.
D6|0.65|A flower with both stamens and carpels is bisexual. A flower with only one of them is unisexual.
Z1|0.35|Question two: symmetry. Imagine cutting a flower through its centre.
Z2|0.75|If every cut through the centre gives two equal halves, the flower is actinomorphic, which means radially symmetrical. Mustard, datura and chilli are examples.
Z3|0.75|If only one cut gives two similar halves, the flower is zygomorphic, which means bilaterally symmetrical. Pea, gulmohur, bean and Cassia are examples.
Z4|0.75|And if no cut gives two similar halves, the flower is asymmetric, or irregular, like canna.
Z5|0.65|Flowers also count their parts. With parts in multiples of three, four or five, they are trimerous, tetramerous or pentamerous.
Z6|0.65|A small reduced leaf at the base of the pedicel is a bract. Flowers with bracts are bracteate. Without them, ebracteate.
P1|0.35|Question three: where does the ovary sit? Watch the thalamus.
P2|0.75|Hypogynous. The gynoecium sits at the highest point, and the other parts are below it. The ovary is superior, as in mustard, china rose and brinjal.
P3|0.75|Perigynous. The thalamus forms a cup. The gynoecium is in the centre, and the other parts sit on the rim, at almost the same level. The ovary is half inferior, as in plum, rose and peach.
P4|0.75|Epigynous. The rim of the thalamus grows up, wraps the ovary completely, and fuses with it. The other parts arise above the ovary. The ovary is inferior, as in guava, cucumber, and the ray florets of sunflower.
C1|0.35|Now the two ends of this scale, side by side.
C2|0.45|Both flowers can have all four whorls, and both keep their ovules inside the ovary.
C3|0.65|But in a hypogynous flower, the ovary is on top, and it is superior. In an epigynous flower, the ovary is below the other parts, and it is inferior.
